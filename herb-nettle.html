Nettle

Urtica dioica

Green • Earthy • Spinach-like

The hedgerow plant every child learns to avoid, and a classic British spring tea. What nettle is, how to make the tea safely, what research says and who should take care.

Explore Nettle ↓ Join The Library

Quick answer

What is nettle tea good for?

Nettle tea is made from the leaves of the stinging nettle, Urtica dioica. Drying or steeping the leaves removes the sting. European regulators accept nettle leaf’s traditional use to increase urine flow and as an add-on for minor joint pain. It tastes green and earthy, like mild spinach. Claims about hay fever and other benefits have little good evidence.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Avoid if you have been told to limit fluids, for example with severe heart or kidney disease.
  • It increases urine; take care with diuretics and blood pressure or diabetes medicines.
  • Not enough data for pregnancy, breastfeeding or children under 12.
  • Wear gloves when picking, and avoid nettles from roadsides or sprayed ground.

Nettle At A Glance

What is nettle?

Stinging nettle is a perennial plant of the nettle family, Urticaceae, covered in fine stinging hairs. It grows across Britain in hedgerows, gardens and woodland edges.

Botanical Name Urtica dioica
Family Urticaceae
Part Used Leaves (also root)
Character Green, earthy, mineral
Caffeine None

Nettle tea, foraging and the evidence

Nettle tea is one of the most popular foraged teas in Britain. The top searches are about how to make it and what it is good for.

Once dried or steeped in hot water, the sting disappears and the leaves make a mild, green tea.

European regulators accept nettle leaf’s traditional use to increase urine and for minor joint pain. Most other claims are not well supported.

01 • Know The Plant

A sting in the leaf. Nothing in the cup.

Stinging nettle grows upright with heart-shaped, toothed leaves covered in tiny hollow hairs.

Each hair works like a needle, injecting histamine, acetylcholine and formic acid when touched. That is the sting.

Heat and drying break those hairs down, so nettle tea, soup and cooked greens do not sting.

The leaves are used for tea. Nettle root is a separate herbal product, studied for prostate symptoms, and is not the same as leaf tea.

Gloves to pick. Hot water to tame. A spring green in a cup.

02 • Nettle Tea Benefits

What is nettle tea good for?

Nettle has a long place in European folk medicine. Regulators accept two traditional uses for the leaf.

The honest picture: two accepted traditional uses, weak evidence for the rest.

The European herbal committee accepts nettle leaf to increase urine flow and for relief of minor joint pain, based on long-standing use. Clinical evidence for hay fever, blood sugar and other popular claims is limited.

Accepted Traditional Use

Increasing urine flow

Nettle leaf is traditionally used to increase the amount of urine to help flush the urinary tract.

Accepted Traditional Use

Minor joint pain

Nettle leaf is accepted as a traditional herbal medicine for relief of minor joint pain.

Limited Evidence

Hay fever

Nettle is popular for hay fever. A few small studies exist, but the evidence is weak and mixed.

04 • British & European Heritage

A hedgerow plant with a long history.

Stinging nettle grows across Europe, Asia and North Africa, and is found almost everywhere in Britain.

People have eaten it, brewed it and even woven it into cloth for thousands of years. Nettle soup and nettle beer were traditional spring foods.

Today nettle is at the centre of the foraging revival, and dried nettle tea is sold in UK supermarkets and health shops.

Native Range

06 • Nettle Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

These are areas researchers have studied. They do not mean nettle tea treats these conditions.

Research Area 01

Joint pain

Studies have looked at nettle leaf taken by mouth and applied to the skin for joint pain. Results are mixed.

Research Area 02

Hay fever

A few small trials have tested freeze-dried nettle for hay fever, with weak and inconsistent results.

Research Area 03

Prostate symptoms

Nettle root, not leaf, has been studied for urinary symptoms from an enlarged prostate.

07 • What Modern Research Says

Accepted tradition. Thin clinical evidence.

Nettle is widely used but has relatively few high-quality human trials for the leaf.

Regulatory Review

Two traditional uses

The EMA herbal committee accepts nettle leaf to increase urine flow and for minor joint pain, based on traditional use.

Key Limitation

Mostly uncontrolled studies

European reviewers noted that published clinical evidence for nettle leaf consists mainly of uncontrolled studies.

Safety Note

Fluid restriction

Nettle leaf is not advised where reduced fluid intake is recommended, such as severe heart or kidney disease.

08 • Three Ways To Read The Evidence

Rooted In Tradition

10 • How To Make Nettle Tea

How to make stinging nettle tea.

From fresh spring tops or dried leaf. Hot water removes the sting.

Fresh or dried nettle tea

Fresh: wearing gloves, pick the top 4 to 6 leaves of young nettles in spring, away from roads and sprayed ground. Rinse well.

Use a small handful of fresh tops, or 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried nettle leaf, per cup.

Pour over about 250 ml of just-boiled water and steep for 5 to 10 minutes. The heat removes the sting.

Strain and drink. A slice of lemon or a little honey brightens the green flavour.

Step 01 • Pick Wear gloves. Take young tops from clean places.
Step 02 • Measure A small handful fresh, or 1–2 teaspoons dried.
Step 03 • Steep In 250 ml of just-boiled water for 5–10 minutes.
Step 04 • Strain Strain and add lemon or honey if you like.

11 • Nettle Side Effects & Safety

Safe in the cup. Careful with medicines.

Fluids, medicines and foraging.

Because nettle leaf increases urine, it is not advised if you have been told to limit fluids, for example with severe heart or kidney disease.

Take care if you use diuretics, or medicines for blood pressure, diabetes or blood clotting, and ask a pharmacist.

Mild stomach upset and, rarely, skin allergy have been reported. There is not enough data for children under 12, pregnancy or breastfeeding.

When foraging, pick only plants you are sure are nettles, from clean places away from roads, dogs and weedkiller.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist or doctor before using nettle medicinally. Ordinary cups of nettle tea are widely enjoyed by healthy adults.

The Rootz Library is educational and does not diagnose, treat or replace medical advice.

12 • Questions People Ask About Nettle

Nettle tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

How do you make nettle tea?
Steep a small handful of fresh young nettle tops, or 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried leaf, in 250 ml of just-boiled water for 5 to 10 minutes, then strain.
How do you make stinging nettle tea from fresh nettles?
Wear gloves, pick the top few leaves of young nettles from a clean spot, rinse, then steep in just-boiled water for 5 to 10 minutes. The heat removes the sting.
What is nettle tea good for?
It is traditionally used to increase urine flow and for minor joint pain. European regulators accept both traditional uses.
Is nettle tea good for you?
For most healthy adults it is a safe, caffeine-free green tea. It is not suitable for people on fluid restrictions.
Does nettle tea help with hay fever?
Some people use it for hay fever, but evidence from small trials is weak and mixed. Antihistamines from a pharmacist are the proven option.
Is nettle tea bad for your kidneys?
For healthy people, nettle tea is not known to harm the kidneys. People with severe kidney or heart disease who must limit fluids should avoid it.
What does nettle tea taste like?
Mild, green and earthy, a bit like spinach or a gentle green tea.
Does nettle tea sting?
No. Drying or steeping in hot water breaks down the stinging hairs.
When should I pick nettles for tea?
In spring, before the plants flower, take the young top leaves. Older plants become tough and gritty.
Does nettle tea have caffeine?
No. Nettle tea is naturally caffeine-free.
