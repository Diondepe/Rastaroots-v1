Honeybush

Cyclopia species

Honeyed • Sweet • Floral

A naturally sweet, caffeine-free tea from the mountains of South Africa’s Cape. What honeybush is, how it differs from rooibos, what research says and how to brew it.

Explore Honeybush ↓ Join The Library

Quick answer

What is honeybush tea?

Honeybush tea is made from the leaves and stems of Cyclopia shrubs, which grow only in the fynbos of South Africa’s Western and Eastern Cape. It is naturally caffeine-free, low in tannins and tastes sweet and honey-like, without added honey. It is related to rooibos but is a different plant. Its antioxidants, especially mangiferin, have been studied in the lab, but human evidence for health benefits is limited.

Side effects & who should take care

  • No side effects have been reported in the research literature, and it is widely drunk as an everyday tea.
  • Human studies are few, so health claims are unproven.
  • Safety data for medicinal or concentrated use in pregnancy and breastfeeding are limited.
  • Concentrated extracts are different from a normal cup of tea.

Honeybush At A Glance

What is honeybush?

Honeybush is the common name for shrubs in the genus Cyclopia, part of the legume family, Fabaceae. Several species are used for tea, including C. intermedia, C. subternata and C. genistoides.

Botanical Name Cyclopia species
Family Fabaceae (legumes)
Parts Used Leaves, stems & flowers
Character Sweet, honeyed, mellow
Native Range Western & Eastern Cape, South Africa

Honeybush tea, rooibos and the fynbos

Honeybush grows only in the fynbos, the unique shrubland of South Africa’s Cape, which is also home to rooibos.

Its name comes from its yellow, honey-scented flowers. In Afrikaans it is heuningbos, and it is also called bergtee, or mountain tea.

People most often ask whether honeybush has caffeine (it does not) and whether it is the same as rooibos (it is not).

01 • Know The Bush

Rooibos’s sweeter cousin.

Honeybush plants are woody shrubs with narrow leaves and bright yellow, pea-like flowers that smell of honey.

There are around 23 Cyclopia species. Only a few are used commercially for tea.

Most honeybush is “fermented”: the cut leaves and stems are oxidised so they turn red-brown and develop a sweet, honeyed flavour. Unfermented “green” honeybush is also made.

Rooibos is Aspalathus linearis, a different plant from the same fynbos region and the same legume family. The two are often blended.

Same fynbos. Same family. A different plant, with honey in its name and its flavour.

02 • Honeybush Tea Benefits

What is honeybush tea good for?

Honeybush is first and foremost a delicious caffeine-free tea. Research has focused on its polyphenols, especially mangiferin and hesperidin.

The honest picture: interesting compounds, little human evidence.

Most honeybush research uses concentrated extracts in the lab or in animals. Reviews of rooibos and honeybush note that evidence about the effects of these herbal teas in people is still limited.

Everyday Benefit

Caffeine-free and low in tannins

Honeybush has no caffeine and is low in tannins, so it suits evenings and people cutting down on caffeine.

Laboratory Research

Mangiferin and hesperidin

Honeybush is a rich source of mangiferin, a plant compound with antioxidant activity studied in the lab and in animals.

Traditionally Associated

Coughs and colds

Honeybush has been used traditionally in the Cape as a soothing drink, including for coughs. This is cultural use, not a proven effect.

04 • South African Heritage

Born in the fynbos. Found nowhere else.

Honeybush grows naturally only in the fynbos of South Africa’s Western and Eastern Cape, one of the richest plant regions on Earth.

It has been used in the Cape for generations, traditionally associated with the indigenous Khoi and San peoples and later with farming communities.

For a long time it was gathered from the wild. Today it is also cultivated, and wild harvesting is managed to protect fynbos plant populations.

Native Range

Fynbos of the Western and Eastern Cape.

Names

Heuningbos, bergtee, mountain tea.

Wild & Farmed

Gathered from hillsides and now cultivated.

Fynbos Family

A close neighbour of rooibos.

06 • Honeybush Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

These are areas researchers have studied, mostly with extracts in the lab or in animals. They do not mean honeybush tea treats these conditions.

Research Area 01

Antioxidant activity

Honeybush polyphenols, especially mangiferin and hesperidin, show antioxidant activity in laboratory studies.

Research Area 02

Blood sugar

Animal studies have looked at honeybush extracts and blood sugar control. Human evidence is lacking.

Research Area 03

Skin and bone

Early lab and small studies have explored skin and bone markers. Results are preliminary.

07 • What Modern Research Says

A healthy-looking profile. Limited human proof.

Honeybush has a promising chemical profile, but most research has not yet moved into people.

Scientific Review

Bioactive compounds identified

A review of South African herbal teas describes honeybush as rich in polyphenols, including the xanthone mangiferin and flavanones such as hesperetin.

Key Limitation

Few human studies

Evidence on the effects of honeybush in people is limited. Most findings come from extracts tested in the lab or in animals.

Everyday Context

A good caffeine swap

The clearest benefit is practical: a naturally sweet, caffeine-free tea you can drink at any time of day.

10 • How To Brew Honeybush Tea

How to brew honeybush tea.

Honeybush is forgiving. Long steeping makes it sweeter, not more bitter.

Simple honeybush tea

Use 1 to 2 teaspoons of loose honeybush, or 1 tea bag, per cup.

Pour over about 250 ml of freshly boiled water.

Steep for at least 5 minutes. Because it is low in tannins, longer steeping deepens the flavour without bitterness.

Drink plain, with milk, or chilled over ice. It is naturally sweet, so most people do not need sugar.

Step 01 • Measure 1–2 teaspoons of loose leaf or 1 tea bag.
Step 02 • Pour About 250 ml of freshly boiled water.
Step 03 • Steep At least 5 minutes; longer is fine.
Step 04 • Serve Plain, with milk, or iced.

11 • Honeybush Side Effects & Safety

Gentle, everyday and caffeine-free.

Generally well tolerated.

Honeybush is widely drunk as an everyday tea, and no side effects have been reported in the research literature.

It contains no caffeine and is low in tannins, which makes it suitable for most people who want a caffeine-free drink.

Human research is limited, so concentrated honeybush extracts and supplements should not be assumed to be as gentle as a cup of tea.

There is limited information on medicinal use in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, check with a pharmacist or doctor before using concentrated honeybush extracts. Ordinary cups of honeybush tea are widely enjoyed as a caffeine-free drink.

The Rootz Library is educational and does not diagnose, treat or replace medical advice.

12 • Questions People Ask About Honeybush

Honeybush questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

What is honeybush tea?
Honeybush tea is a naturally sweet, caffeine-free herbal tea made from the leaves and stems of Cyclopia shrubs, which grow only in the fynbos of South Africa’s Cape.
Does honeybush tea have caffeine?
No. Honeybush tea is naturally caffeine-free.
Is honeybush the same as rooibos?
No. Honeybush comes from Cyclopia species and rooibos from Aspalathus linearis. Both are fynbos plants in the legume family and are often blended, but they are different plants.
What does honeybush tea taste like?
It tastes sweet, honeyed and floral, with a mellow, smooth finish. It is sweeter and lighter than rooibos.
Is honeybush tea good for you?
It is a caffeine-free, low-tannin drink rich in polyphenols such as mangiferin. Lab studies are interesting, but there is little human evidence for specific health benefits.
What is honeybush good for?
It is mainly enjoyed as a caffeine-free everyday tea. Traditionally it was used as a soothing drink, including for coughs, but this is not proven.
Is honeybush tea safe during pregnancy?
Honeybush is widely drunk as an everyday caffeine-free tea, but there is limited research in pregnancy. Avoid concentrated extracts and ask your midwife if unsure.
Where does honeybush come from?
Honeybush grows naturally only in the Western and Eastern Cape of South Africa.
How long should I steep honeybush tea?
At least 5 minutes. Because it is low in tannins, it does not turn bitter with longer steeping.
Where can I buy honeybush tea in the UK?
Honeybush is sold in many UK supermarkets, health food shops and tea retailers, as tea bags and loose leaf.
