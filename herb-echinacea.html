Echinacea

Echinacea purpurea

The purple coneflower famous as a cold remedy. What the best research really shows, how to make echinacea tea and who should avoid it.

Quick answer

Does echinacea work for colds?

Echinacea (purple coneflower) is a North American wildflower widely used for colds. The evidence is inconsistent: a large Cochrane review of 24 trials found that some products might slightly reduce the chance of catching a cold, but did not show a clear effect on how long colds last. Products vary a lot, and tea has been studied far less than extracts.

Side effects & who should take care

  • People allergic to ragweed, daisies, marigolds or chrysanthemums may react.
  • Avoid it with autoimmune conditions or if you take immune-suppressing medicines.
  • Use it short-term only.
  • Ask a midwife or GP before using it in pregnancy.

Echinacea At A Glance

What is echinacea?

Echinacea is a tall prairie wildflower in the daisy family, with purple petals around a spiky orange-brown cone.

Family Asteraceae (daisy family)
Part Used Flowers, leaves and roots

01 • Know The Flower

A spiky cone, a famous remedy.

Echinacea’s name comes from the Greek for hedgehog, after its spiky central cone.

Three species are used: E. purpurea, E. angustifolia and E. pallida. They may work differently.

Products may use the flowers, leaves or roots, fresh or dried, so they vary a lot.

Echinacea root often makes the tongue tingle.

A prairie flower with a big reputation and mixed results.

02 • Echinacea Benefits

What is echinacea good for?

Echinacea is sold to support the immune system during colds.

The honest picture: inconsistent, at best a small effect.

A 2014 Cochrane review of 24 trials with over 4,600 people found that some echinacea products might slightly reduce the chance of catching a cold, but the evidence was weak and there was no clear effect on how long colds last. Products differed widely. There is not enough evidence for other uses.

European settlers adopted it, and today it is one of the most popular herbal cold remedies in Europe and the UK.

10 • How To Make Echinacea Tea

How to make echinacea tea.

Use flowers and leaves for a gentle cup, root for a stronger one.

Echinacea tea

Put 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried echinacea flowers and leaves in a cup, or simmer 1 teaspoon of dried root in 300 ml of water for 10 minutes.

For flowers, pour over 250 ml of just-boiled water.

Cover and steep for 10 minutes, then strain.

Add honey, lemon or ginger. Use it for short periods only.

Step 01 • Measure 1–2 tsp flowers or 1 tsp root.
Step 02 • Pour 250 ml just-boiled water.
Step 03 • Steep 10 minutes, covered.
Step 04 • Limit Short-term use.

11 • Echinacea Side Effects & Safety

Generally well tolerated, with cautions.

Allergies, autoimmune conditions and medicines.

Echinacea is generally well tolerated short-term. It can cause stomach upset or a rash.

Allergies: people allergic to ragweed, chrysanthemums, marigolds or daisies are more likely to react, sometimes seriously.

Immune conditions: avoid it if you have an autoimmune condition, such as lupus or rheumatoid arthritis, or take immune-suppressing medicines.

Pregnancy and children: ask a midwife or GP before using it in pregnancy, and check product labels for children’s age limits.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist before using echinacea with regular medicines or if you have an immune condition.

12 • Questions People Ask About Echinacea

Echinacea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Does echinacea work for colds?
The evidence is inconsistent. Some products might slightly reduce the chance of a cold, but they do not clearly shorten colds.
Can echinacea prevent a cold?
A Cochrane review found some products might slightly reduce the risk, but the evidence is weak.
How do you make echinacea tea?
Steep 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried flowers and leaves in 250 ml of just-boiled water for 10 minutes, covered.
What are the side effects of echinacea?
Stomach upset and rashes. Allergic reactions can happen, especially in people allergic to the daisy family.
Who should avoid echinacea?
People with daisy-family allergies, autoimmune conditions or on immune-suppressing medicines.
How long can I take echinacea?
It is usually used for short periods. Follow the product label.
Is echinacea safe in pregnancy?
Ask a midwife or GP first.
Does echinacea tea contain caffeine?
No. Echinacea tea is caffeine-free.
Why does echinacea make my tongue tingle?
Compounds in the root, called alkylamides, cause a tingling feeling.
Where does echinacea come from?
It is native to the prairies of eastern and central North America.
