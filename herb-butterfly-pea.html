Butterfly Pea

Clitoria ternatea

Earthy • Woody • Mild

The blue tea that turns purple with a squeeze of lemon. How to make butterfly pea flower tea, why it changes colour, what research really shows, and its life as “blue pea” in Jamaica.

Quick answer

Is butterfly pea tea good for you?

Butterfly pea tea, or blue tea, is made from the dried flowers of Clitoria ternatea. It is caffeine-free, mild and earthy, and famous for its deep blue colour, which turns purple with lemon or lime. The colour comes from anthocyanins, studied in the lab, but there are few human trials, so health claims are not proven.

Side effects & who should take care

  • A cup a day is likely fine for most adults.
  • Not enough is known about safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding; avoid concentrated extracts.
  • Ask a pharmacist if you take blood thinners or diabetes medicines.
  • Buy flowers sold for food.

Butterfly Pea At A Glance

What is butterfly pea?

Butterfly pea is a tropical climbing vine in the pea family with vivid blue flowers. In Jamaica and the Bahamas it is called blue pea.

Botanical Name Clitoria ternatea
Family Fabaceae (pea family)
Part Used Flowers
Character Mild, earthy, blue
Common Names Blue pea, blue tea, anchan, bunga telang

Butterfly pea, the Caribbean and the evidence

Butterfly pea is used in Thailand, Malaysia and India for tea, rice and desserts.

It grows across the Caribbean, where it is called blue pea in Jamaica and the Bahamas.

Its colour-changing pigments are studied in the lab. Human evidence for health benefits is very limited.

01 • Know The Flower

A blue flower that changes colour.

Butterfly pea is a climbing vine with bright blue, pea-like flowers. It is a legume, so it improves the soil it grows in.

The flowers are dried and steeped to make a deep blue tea.

Its blue comes from anthocyanins, natural pigments that change colour with acidity. Add lemon or lime and the tea turns purple or pink.

The flavour is mild and earthy, so it is often blended with lemongrass, ginger or honey.

Blue in the pot. Purple with lemon. Pure kitchen chemistry.

02 • Butterfly Pea Benefits

What is butterfly pea tea good for?

Butterfly pea is popular for its colour. Its health claims are mostly from the lab.

The honest picture: a beautiful, caffeine-free drink, not a remedy.

Butterfly pea flowers contain anthocyanins called ternatins, which show antioxidant activity in laboratory and animal studies. There are very few human trials, so claims about memory, skin or blood sugar are not proven. Enjoy it as a fun, caffeine-free drink.

04 • Caribbean & World Heritage

From Southeast Asia to Caribbean fences.

Butterfly pea is thought to have originated in tropical Asia, where it has long been used to colour rice, desserts and drinks in Thailand, Malaysia and Indonesia.

It spread across the tropics, including Africa and the Caribbean.

In the West Indies it is an introduced vine, known as blue pea in Jamaica and the Bahamas and as conchitas in Cuba, Puerto Rico and the Dominican Republic, often climbing garden fences.

10 • How To Make Butterfly Pea Tea

How to make butterfly pea tea.

Add the lemon at the end, in front of your guests, to see the colour change.

Colour-changing blue tea

Put 5 to 6 dried butterfly pea flowers (about 1 teaspoon) in a cup or teapot.

Pour over 250 ml of just-boiled water and steep for 5 minutes until deep blue.

Strain, and sweeten with a little honey if you like.

Squeeze in some lemon or lime juice and watch it turn purple.

11 • Butterfly Pea Side Effects & Safety

A fun cup, with a few unknowns.

Pregnancy, medicines and extracts.

Butterfly pea flower tea is widely used as a food drink and is likely fine for most adults in normal amounts.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding: there is not enough research on safety, so avoid concentrated extracts and ask your midwife about regular tea.

Medicines: if you take blood thinners or diabetes medicines, ask a pharmacist before drinking it often, as research on interactions is limited.

Buy food-grade flowers from a trusted supplier.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist before using butterfly pea supplements or extracts, especially if you are pregnant or take regular medicines.

12 • Questions People Ask About Butterfly Pea

Butterfly pea tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Is butterfly pea tea good for you?
It is a caffeine-free, colourful drink. Its antioxidant pigments are studied in the lab, but human evidence for health benefits is limited.
What is blue tea?
Blue tea is another name for butterfly pea flower tea, made from the flowers of Clitoria ternatea.
Why does butterfly pea tea change colour?
Its anthocyanin pigments react to acidity. Lemon or lime juice turns the blue tea purple or pink.
How do you make butterfly pea tea?
Steep 5 to 6 dried flowers in 250 ml of just-boiled water for 5 minutes, strain, then add lemon to change the colour.
What does butterfly pea tea taste like?
Mild, earthy and slightly woody. It is often blended with lemongrass, ginger or honey.
Does butterfly pea tea contain caffeine?
No. Pure butterfly pea tea is naturally caffeine-free.
Is butterfly pea tea safe in pregnancy?
There is not enough research. Avoid extracts and ask your midwife before drinking it regularly.
Does butterfly pea grow in Jamaica?
Yes. It is an introduced vine across the Caribbean and is called blue pea in Jamaica and the Bahamas.
Does butterfly pea tea help memory?
It is used in Ayurvedic tradition, but there is no good human evidence that it improves memory.
Can I drink butterfly pea tea every day?
A cup a day is generally fine for most adults. Ask a pharmacist if you take regular medicines.
