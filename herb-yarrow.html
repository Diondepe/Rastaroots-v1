Quick answer

What is yarrow tea good for?

Yarrow (Achillea millefolium) is a common wildflower with feathery leaves and flat white flower heads. European regulators recognise its traditional use for temporary loss of appetite and mild digestive problems such as bloating and wind, for minor period cramps, and on small superficial wounds. This is based on long use, not strong trials.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Avoid it in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • People allergic to daisies, ragweed or chrysanthemums may react.
  • See a doctor if digestive symptoms last more than two weeks.
  • Medicinal products are for adults.

Yarrow At A Glance

What is yarrow?

Yarrow is a hardy wildflower in the daisy family, found in grassland and lawns across Britain.

Part Used Flowering tops

Yarrow, Achilles and the evidence

Yarrow grows wild across Britain, Europe, Asia and North America.

Its name Achillea comes from the Greek hero Achilles, said to have used it on soldiers’ wounds.

European regulators recognise its traditional use for appetite and mild digestive problems.

01 • Know The Herb

A thousand leaves.

Yarrow has finely divided, feathery leaves, which gave it the name millefolium, “a thousand leaves”.

Its flat-topped clusters of tiny flowers are usually white, sometimes pink.

The above-ground flowering parts are dried for tea.

It tastes bitter and aromatic, which is part of its traditional use for appetite.

A thousand leaves, a hero’s name, a bitter cup.

02 • Yarrow Benefits

What is yarrow good for?

Yarrow has several recognised traditional uses in Europe.

The honest picture: recognised traditional use, limited trials.

The European Medicines Agency’s herbal committee concluded that, based on long-standing use, yarrow preparations can be used for temporary loss of appetite and mild digestive problems including bloating and flatulence, for minor period cramps, and on small superficial wounds. Lab studies show antibacterial and anti-spasm effects, but clinical trials are limited.

10 • How To Make Yarrow Tea

How to make yarrow tea.

A bitter cup, best before meals.

Yarrow tea

Put 1 teaspoon of dried yarrow in a cup.

Pour over 250 ml of just-boiled water.

Cover and steep for 5 to 10 minutes, then strain.

Drink it before meals, with honey or mint if you like.

Step 01 • Measure 1 tsp dried yarrow.
Step 02 • Pour 250 ml just-boiled water.
Step 03 • Steep 5–10 minutes, covered.
Step 04 • Timing Before meals.

11 • Yarrow Side Effects & Safety

A gentle bitter, with cautions.

Pregnancy, allergies and when to see a doctor.

Yarrow tea is generally well tolerated by adults.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding: avoid it.

Allergies: people allergic to daisies, ragweed, chrysanthemums or other daisy-family plants may react, including skin rashes.

When to see a doctor: if digestive symptoms last more than two weeks, if period cramps or wounds do not improve within a week, or if a wound looks infected.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist before using yarrow with regular medicines.

The Rootz Library is educational and does not diagnose, treat or replace medical advice.

It has recognised traditional use for appetite and mild digestive problems, plus minor period cramps.
How do you make yarrow tea?

Why is it called Achillea?
After the Greek hero Achilles, said to have used it on wounds.
Is yarrow safe in pregnancy?
No. Avoid it.
Can you be allergic to yarrow?
Yes, especially if you are allergic to daisy-family plants.
Does yarrow tea taste bitter?
Yes. It is bitter and aromatic.
Can I use yarrow on cuts?
It has traditional use on small superficial wounds. See a doctor for deep or infected wounds.
Does yarrow tea contain caffeine?
No. It is caffeine-free.
Where does yarrow grow?
In grassland, verges and lawns across Britain and the northern hemisphere.

“A thousand leaves”, from its feathery foliage.
