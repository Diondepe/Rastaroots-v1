What counts as herbal tea?

Herbal tea, or a tisane, is an infusion made from plants other than the traditional tea plant, Camellia sinensis. Depending on the blend, it might use flowers, leaves, roots, fruits or seeds. That gives you a wide range of flavours—from bright and floral to earthy, minty and bitter.

Tea versus infusion

Black, green and breakfast teas come from Camellia sinensis. Botanical infusions use other plants, such as ginger, mint and hibiscus. Neither is automatically “better”; they offer different tastes and drinking experiences.

Does herbal tea have caffeine?

Many infusions made only from naturally caffeine-free botanicals contain no added caffeine ingredients. Always check the full blend label, especially if you need to avoid caffeine strictly.

How do I choose?

Start with flavour. Ginger feels warming and spicy, hibiscus is tart and floral, mint tastes fresh, and roots can give a deeper earthy character. Then choose a ritual that suits your day.

Herbal tea UK: common questions

What is the difference between herbal tea and breakfast tea?

Breakfast tea is generally made from leaves of Camellia sinensis and contains caffeine. Herbal tea uses other botanicals, such as flowers, leaves or roots.

Are RastaRoots teas caffeine-free?
