Ginseng

Panax ginseng

Earthy • Bittersweet • Woody

The famous root of Korea and China. How to make ginseng tea, the difference between Korean, American and “Siberian” ginseng, what the research really shows, and why it is not for everyone.

Quick answer

Is ginseng tea good for you?

Ginseng tea is made from the root of Panax ginseng (Korean or Asian ginseng) or Panax quinquefolius (American ginseng). It is promoted for energy, focus and blood sugar, but the evidence is mixed and mostly from supplements, not tea. Its most common side effect is trouble sleeping, and it may interact with diabetes medicines and warfarin. It is not advised in pregnancy.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Drink it earlier in the day: insomnia is the most common side effect.
  • Avoid during pregnancy and breastfeeding, and do not give it to children.
  • Ask a pharmacist first if you take diabetes medicines, warfarin or antidepressants.
  • “Siberian ginseng” is a different plant, not true ginseng.

Ginseng At A Glance

What is ginseng?

Ginseng is the slow-growing root of plants in the Panax genus, prized in Korean and Chinese tradition for centuries.

Botanical Names Panax ginseng (Asian); Panax quinquefolius (American)
Family Araliaceae (ivy family)
Part Used Root
Character Earthy, bittersweet
Key Compounds Ginsenosides

Ginseng, tradition and the evidence

Ginseng has been treasured in Korea and China for centuries, and American ginseng was later traded from North America to Asia.

Red ginseng is steamed and dried; white ginseng is simply dried.

Research on energy, focus and blood sugar is inconsistent. It is a stimulating root, so it is not a bedtime tea.

01 • Know The Root

A forked root with a long story.

Ginseng is the fleshy root of plants in the Panax genus. Its forked shape, sometimes like a little figure, helped make it famous.

Asian ginseng (Panax ginseng), also called Korean or Chinese ginseng, is sold as white ginseng (dried) or red ginseng (steamed, then dried).

American ginseng (Panax quinquefolius) grows in eastern North America.

“Siberian ginseng” (Eleutherococcus senticosus) is a different plant with different chemistry. Labels matter.

Same name on the box. Different roots inside. Read the label.

02 • Ginseng Benefits

What is ginseng tea good for?

Ginseng is marketed for energy, focus and blood sugar. The research is mixed.

The honest picture: interesting research, no clear answers.

Ginseng has been studied for blood sugar, thinking, fatigue and more. A 2022 review found improvements in some markers in people with prediabetes and diabetes, but overall the evidence on blood sugar control is inconclusive and conflicting. Most studies use supplements, not tea. It is not a treatment for diabetes or tiredness.

04 • World Heritage

A root that crossed oceans.

Asian ginseng grows wild in the mountain forests of Korea and northeast China, where it has been prized for centuries. Wild roots became so rare that ginseng is now mostly farmed.

In the 1700s, American ginseng was discovered in North America and shipped to China, becoming one of the early trade links between the two continents.

Today ginseng is sold worldwide, from Korean tea shops to energy drinks on UK shelves.

10 • How To Make Ginseng Tea

How to make ginseng tea.

Ginseng is a root, so it needs a gentle simmer rather than a quick steep.

Ginseng slice tea

Put 3 to 5 thin slices of dried ginseng (about 2 g) in a small pan with 300 ml of water.

Bring to a gentle simmer and cook for 10 to 15 minutes.

Strain into a mug. Add honey or a slice of ginger if you like.

Drink it in the morning or early afternoon, not at night.

11 • Ginseng Side Effects & Safety

A strong root. Use it with care.

Sleep, medicines and pregnancy.

Ginseng tea in small amounts is fine for many healthy adults for short periods.

Sleep: insomnia is the most common side effect. Avoid it in the evening.

Medicines: ginseng may lower blood sugar, so it can add to diabetes medicines. Studies on warfarin have had mixed results, and it may interact with some antidepressants (MAOIs). Ask a pharmacist first.

Pregnancy, breastfeeding and children: Asian ginseng may be unsafe in pregnancy, as one of its compounds caused birth defects in animals. Avoid it if you are pregnant or breastfeeding, and do not give it to children.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist or doctor before using ginseng if you take diabetes medicines, blood thinners or antidepressants, or have a heart condition or high blood pressure.

12 • Questions People Ask About Ginseng

Ginseng tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Is ginseng tea good for you?
Ginseng is studied for energy, focus and blood sugar, but results are mixed and mostly come from supplements. Enjoy it occasionally, earlier in the day.
What are the benefits of ginseng?
Ginseng has been researched for blood sugar, thinking and fatigue. Some studies show small benefits and others none, so no benefit is proven.
How do you make ginseng tea?
Simmer 3 to 5 thin slices of dried ginseng (about 2 g) in 300 ml of water for 10 to 15 minutes, then strain.
What is Korean ginseng tea?
Korean ginseng tea, insam-cha, is made from Panax ginseng root, often sweetened with honey or jujube.
Does ginseng tea keep you awake?
It can. Insomnia is ginseng’s most common side effect, so avoid it in the evening.
Is ginseng safe in pregnancy?
No. Asian ginseng may be unsafe in pregnancy and is best avoided while pregnant or breastfeeding.
Does ginseng interact with medicines?
It may affect blood sugar and has been studied with warfarin and some antidepressants. Ask a pharmacist if you take regular medication.
Is Siberian ginseng real ginseng?
No. Siberian ginseng (Eleutherococcus senticosus) is a different plant from Panax ginseng.
What is the difference between red and white ginseng?
White ginseng is simply dried. Red ginseng is steamed, then dried, which changes its colour and some of its compounds.
Does ginseng tea contain caffeine?
No, pure ginseng tea is caffeine-free, but it can still feel stimulating. Check blends for added green or black tea.
