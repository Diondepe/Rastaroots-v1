Raspberry Leaf

Rubus idaeus

Earthy • Mellow • Black-tea-like

Britain’s best-known pregnancy tea. What raspberry leaf tea is, what the evidence really says about labour, what UK experts advise and who should avoid it.

Explore Raspberry Leaf ↓ Join The Library

Quick answer

What does raspberry leaf tea do, and when should you start?

Raspberry leaf tea is made from the dried leaves of the raspberry plant, Rubus idaeus. Many people drink it late in pregnancy believing it tones the womb for labour. Research has not shown that it starts or shortens labour, and the NHS says there is no proven way to bring on labour at home. If you want to try it, talk to your midwife first; UK pregnancy charity guidance suggests not starting before about 32 weeks.

Who should avoid it or check first

  • Always check with your midwife or doctor before using it in pregnancy.
  • Tommy’s advises against it if you have had a caesarean in the last 2 years, have a planned caesarean, or have high blood pressure or pre-eclampsia.
  • If advised it is fine, Tommy’s suggests starting with 1 cup a day and slowly increasing to no more than 3.
  • Stop and speak to your midwife if you feel unwell or have strong contractions.

Raspberry Leaf At A Glance

What is raspberry leaf tea?

Raspberry leaf tea is an infusion of the dried leaves of the garden and wild raspberry, Rubus idaeus, in the rose family.

Botanical Name Rubus idaeus
Family Rosaceae
Part Used Leaves
Character Mellow, earthy, tannic
Native Range Europe & northern Asia

Raspberry leaf tea, pregnancy and the evidence

Raspberry leaf tea is one of the most widely used herbal teas in British pregnancy, often recommended by friends, family and forums.

The most searched questions are about when to start and whether it induces labour. The honest answer is that research has not shown it brings on labour, and safety evidence is reassuring but limited.

In 2024, the UK’s Committee on Toxicity concluded that the risk from raspberry leaf in pregnancy is low, but with high uncertainty because the data are limited.

01 • Know The Leaf

Not the fruit. The leaf.

Raspberry leaf tea is made from the leaves of the raspberry plant, not the berries. It does not taste of raspberries.

The flavour is mellow and earthy, a little like a light black tea, because the leaves are rich in tannins.

Some products are labelled red raspberry leaf. This usually means the same plant, Rubus idaeus, or the closely related American red raspberry.

It is naturally caffeine-free when made only from raspberry leaves.

A berry plant. A leaf tea. A tradition that research has not yet caught up with.

02 • Raspberry Leaf Tea Benefits

What does raspberry leaf tea do?

Raspberry leaf has a long tradition as a “womb tonic” in late pregnancy. The research has tested that idea, and the results are underwhelming.

The biggest myth: “raspberry leaf tea brings on labour.”

No study has shown that raspberry leaf tea starts labour. NHS maternity units list it under “busting the myths”. The tradition is that it may help the womb work more efficiently once labour begins, and even that is unproven.

Human Research

Length of labour

A small Australian trial of raspberry leaf tablets found no statistically significant shortening of labour, although the second stage was a little shorter on average.

Traditionally Associated

A late-pregnancy tonic

Midwives and families have used raspberry leaf for generations to “prepare” the womb. That tradition is strong; the evidence for it is weak.

Outside Pregnancy

A caffeine-free cuppa

Many people simply enjoy it as a mellow, caffeine-free alternative to black tea.

04 • British & European Heritage

A hedgerow plant turned maternity tradition.

Raspberries grow wild across Britain and Europe and have been cultivated in gardens for centuries.

European herbalists used the leaves long before modern medicine, and raspberry leaf became closely tied to midwifery and pregnancy care.

Today it is sold in supermarkets, chemists and health food shops across the UK, and is one of the most searched-for herbal teas in Britain.

06 • Raspberry Leaf Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

These are the questions researchers have asked. They do not mean raspberry leaf tea has proven effects.

Research Area 01

Length and progress of labour

Studies have measured whether raspberry leaf shortens labour or reduces the need for interventions. Results are inconclusive.

Research Area 02

Safety for mother and baby

Human studies have not found harm to mothers or babies, apart from possible constipation, but they are small.

Research Area 03

Effects on womb muscle

Animal and lab studies show raspberry leaf can affect smooth muscle, but how this might work in labour is poorly understood.

07 • What Modern Research Says

No proven benefit. Reassuring but limited safety data.

Raspberry leaf has more human research than many herbs, but the studies are small and old. Reviews agree the evidence is weak.

Systematic Review

Evidence base is weak

A 2021 systematic review found human studies have not shown any harm or benefit, and called the evidence supporting its use in pregnancy weak.

UK Committee On Toxicity

Low risk, high uncertainty

The COT concluded in 2024 that the risk from raspberry leaf in pregnancy is low but uncertain, partly because UK use may be far higher than tested doses.

NHS Position

No proven way to start labour

The NHS says there is no evidence that foods, drinks or herbal supplements start labour, and herbal products have not been checked for safety.

08 • Three Ways To Read The Evidence

Tradition. Science. The unknown.

Rooted In Tradition

A trusted family tradition.

Generations of women have used raspberry leaf in pregnancy. That experience matters and deserves to be taken seriously.

Studied By Science

It has been tested.

Small studies show no clear harm and no clear benefit for labour. The research is limited but real.

Not Yet Proven

It does not induce labour.

There is no evidence that raspberry leaf tea brings on labour, and it should never replace care from your midwife.

10 • How To Make Raspberry Leaf Tea

How to make raspberry leaf tea.

A simple cup. If you are pregnant, agree with your midwife first whether and when to drink it.

Simple raspberry leaf tea

Use 1 teaspoon of dried raspberry leaf or 1 tea bag per cup.

Pour over about 250 ml of freshly boiled water.

Cover and steep for 5 to 10 minutes, then strain. It tastes mellow and earthy; a little honey or lemon is optional.

In pregnancy, follow your midwife’s advice. Tommy’s suggests starting with 1 cup a day and building slowly to no more than 3.

11 • Raspberry Leaf Side Effects & Safety

Common does not mean proven.

Pregnancy is the key consideration.

Raspberry leaf is mostly used in pregnancy, so safety matters more than for an everyday herbal tea.

Human studies have not found harm to mothers or babies, but they are small. The UK Committee on Toxicity describes the risk as low but uncertain.

Pregnancy charity Tommy’s advises not to take raspberry leaf tea or tablets if you have had a caesarean in the last 2 years, are having a planned caesarean, or have high blood pressure or pre-eclampsia.

If your midwife agrees you can try it, Tommy’s suggests starting no earlier than about 32 weeks, with 1 cup a day increasing slowly to 3. Tablets and tinctures are usually stronger than tea.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, talk to your midwife or doctor before using raspberry leaf in any form, and stop if you feel unwell or have strong or frequent contractions.

12 • Questions People Ask About Raspberry Leaf

Raspberry leaf tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

When should I start drinking raspberry leaf tea?
Talk to your midwife first. If they agree it is suitable for you, UK pregnancy charity Tommy’s suggests not starting before about 32 weeks, beginning with 1 cup a day and slowly increasing to 3.
When to start drinking raspberry leaf tea according to the NHS?
The NHS does not recommend raspberry leaf tea or give a start date. It says there is no proven way to bring on labour at home and that herbal supplements have not been checked for safety. Ask your midwife.
What does raspberry leaf tea do?
It is traditionally believed to tone the womb so contractions work more efficiently in labour. Research has not proven this. It is also a mellow, caffeine-free tea.
Does raspberry leaf tea induce labour?
No. There is no evidence that raspberry leaf tea starts labour, and NHS maternity units list it as a myth. Some people believe it may help once labour has started, but that is unproven.
Does raspberry leaf tea work?
Studies have not shown a clear benefit. One small trial found a slightly shorter second stage of labour on average, but the difference was not statistically significant.
Is raspberry leaf tea safe in pregnancy?
Small studies have not found harm, and UK toxicology experts describe the risk as low but uncertain. It is not suitable for everyone, so check with your midwife first.
Who should not drink raspberry leaf tea?
Tommy’s advises against it if you have had a caesarean in the last 2 years, have a planned caesarean, or have high blood pressure or pre-eclampsia. Ask your midwife about any other complications.
How much raspberry leaf tea should I drink?
If your midwife agrees, Tommy’s suggests 1 cup a day increasing slowly to no more than 3. Tablets are usually a stronger dose than tea.
Can I drink raspberry leaf tea if I am not pregnant?
Yes, many people enjoy it as a mellow, caffeine-free herbal tea. Speak to a pharmacist if you take regular medicines.
Does raspberry leaf tea taste like raspberries?
No. It is made from the leaves, not the fruit, and tastes earthy and mellow, similar to a light black tea.
