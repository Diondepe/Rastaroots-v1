Peppermint

Mentha × piperita

Cooling • Sharp • Menthol

Britain’s favourite after-dinner herbal tea. What peppermint is, whether it helps bloating, what research really says and who should avoid it.

Quick answer

Is peppermint tea good for you?

Peppermint tea is a cooling, naturally caffeine-free infusion of Mentha × piperita leaves. European regulators accept its long traditional use for indigestion and wind, and many people drink it for bloating. Concentrated peppermint oil capsules have good trial evidence for IBS symptoms, but that does not automatically apply to tea. For most people it is a safe everyday cup, but it can make heartburn and acid reflux worse.

Side effects & who should take care

  • It can worsen heartburn and acid reflux (GORD).
  • Take care if you have gallstones or other bile problems.
  • EU herbal medicines made from peppermint leaf are for adults and children over 4.
  • Peppermint oil is far stronger than tea; never give it to babies or young children.

Peppermint At A Glance

What is peppermint?

Peppermint is a natural hybrid of watermint and spearmint, in the mint family, Lamiaceae. It is grown worldwide for its leaves and oil.

Botanical Name Mentha × piperita
Family Lamiaceae (mint family)
Part Used Leaves
Character Cooling, sharp, menthol
Caffeine None

Peppermint tea, digestion and the evidence

Peppermint tea is one of the UK’s most popular herbal teas. The top questions are whether it is good for you, whether it has caffeine and whether it helps bloating.

It has no caffeine. European regulators accept its traditional use for indigestion and wind, and its strongest clinical evidence is for concentrated peppermint oil in IBS.

It is not right for everyone: peppermint relaxes the muscle at the top of the stomach, which can make acid reflux worse.

01 • Know The Leaf

A natural hybrid with a menthol kick.

Peppermint grows as a spreading plant with square, purple-tinged stems and dark green, pointed, toothed leaves.

It is a natural cross between watermint (Mentha aquatica) and spearmint (Mentha spicata), which is why it has more bite than spearmint.

Its sharp, cooling character comes from menthol, the main compound in its essential oil. Spearmint has much less menthol and tastes sweeter.

The Library’s Mint entry covers spearmint, the gentler cousin used in many Caribbean and Moroccan teas.

Spearmint is sweet. Peppermint has bite. The difference is menthol.

02 • Peppermint Tea Benefits

What is peppermint tea good for?

Peppermint is one of the better-researched herbal remedies, but most of the strong evidence is for concentrated oil, not tea.

The key distinction: peppermint oil capsules are not peppermint tea.

A 2019 meta-analysis of 12 randomised trials found enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules improved global IBS symptoms and abdominal pain compared with placebo. A cup of tea contains far less oil. Tea is traditionally used for indigestion and wind, which regulators accept based on long-standing use.

Accepted Traditional Use

Indigestion and wind

The European Medicines Agency’s herbal committee concluded peppermint leaf can be used to relieve indigestion and flatulence based on long-standing use.

Strong Human Evidence

Peppermint oil and IBS

Randomised trials show enteric-coated peppermint oil capsules can ease IBS pain and overall symptoms in adults.

Everyday Benefit

A caffeine-free cup

Peppermint tea has no caffeine, making it a popular evening swap for black tea or coffee.

04 • British & European Heritage

A garden hybrid with an English story.

Peppermint was recorded in England in the late 17th century as a distinct mint, and it was soon grown commercially.

Mitcham in Surrey became famous for peppermint oil in the 18th and 19th centuries, and the “Black Mitcham” variety is still grown.

Today peppermint is grown worldwide, and peppermint tea is a staple in UK supermarkets, cafés and kitchen cupboards.

06 • Peppermint Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

These are areas researchers have studied. They do not mean peppermint tea treats these conditions.

Research Area 01

Irritable bowel syndrome

Peppermint oil capsules have been tested in many randomised trials for IBS pain and symptoms.

Research Area 02

Indigestion

Peppermint relaxes smooth muscle in the gut, which may explain its traditional use for fullness and wind.

Research Area 03

Tension headache

Peppermint oil applied to the forehead has been studied for headache. This is a topical use, not tea.

07 • What Modern Research Says

Strong for oil. Traditional for tea.

Peppermint is a good example of why the form matters. The best evidence is for a specific, concentrated product.

Meta-analysis

IBS: positive

A 2019 meta-analysis of 12 trials with 835 patients found peppermint oil improved IBS symptoms and abdominal pain versus placebo.

Regulatory Review

Indigestion: traditional use

The EMA accepts peppermint leaf for indigestion and wind based on traditional use, not on clinical trials of tea.

Key Limitation

Tea ≠ capsule

A cup of tea contains much less oil than a capsule, and is not coated to reach the intestine.

08 • Three Ways To Read The Evidence

Tradition. Science. The unknown.

Rooted In Tradition

A trusted after-dinner cup.

Peppermint has been drunk for digestion for centuries, and European regulators recognise that traditional use.

Studied By Science

The oil is well studied.

Peppermint oil capsules have good randomised-trial evidence for IBS symptoms in adults.

Not Yet Proven

Tea is not a proven treatment.

There are no good trials showing peppermint tea treats IBS, reflux or any disease.

10 • How To Make Peppermint Tea

How to make peppermint tea.

Simple, quick and forgiving. Cover the cup to keep the menthol in.

Fresh or dried peppermint tea

Use a small handful (about 8–10) of fresh leaves, lightly crushed, or 1 teaspoon of dried leaf or 1 tea bag per cup.

Pour over about 250 ml of water just off the boil.

Cover and steep for 5 to 7 minutes. Covering stops the cooling oils escaping with the steam.

Strain and drink plain, or chill it for iced peppermint tea.

11 • Peppermint Side Effects & Safety

Gentle for most. Not for everyone.

Reflux, gallstones and young children.

Peppermint relaxes the muscle between the stomach and the food pipe. That can make heartburn and acid reflux worse. If you have GORD, peppermint may not suit you.

The European assessment advises caution for people with gallstones and other bile problems.

Peppermint leaf herbal medicines are for adults and children over 4. Peppermint oil should never be put on or near the face of babies and young children.

See a doctor if digestive symptoms last more than two weeks, or if you have weight loss, bleeding or difficulty swallowing.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist before using peppermint oil capsules, which can interact with some medicines, including antacids.

12 • Questions People Ask About Peppermint

Peppermint tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Is peppermint tea good for you?
For most people, yes. It is caffeine-free, has no calories when drunk plain, and is traditionally used for indigestion and wind. It may not suit people with acid reflux.
Does peppermint tea have caffeine?
No. Pure peppermint tea is naturally caffeine-free. Blends that include black or green tea do contain caffeine.
What is peppermint tea good for?
It is traditionally used to ease indigestion, fullness and wind after meals. European regulators accept this traditional use.
Does peppermint tea help with bloating?
Many people find it helps, and it is traditionally used for wind and bloating. Peppermint oil capsules have stronger evidence for IBS-related bloating and pain than tea.
Is peppermint tea good for acid reflux?
Usually not. Peppermint can relax the valve at the top of the stomach and make reflux and heartburn worse.
Does peppermint tea help constipation?
There is no good evidence that peppermint tea relieves constipation. Fluids, fibre and activity are the first steps; see a GP if it persists.
Is peppermint tea good for weight loss?
Not directly. It is calorie-free, so it can replace sugary drinks, but it does not burn fat.
What is the difference between peppermint and spearmint?
Peppermint is a hybrid of watermint and spearmint with much more menthol, so it tastes sharper and cooler. Spearmint is sweeter and milder.
Can I drink peppermint tea every day?
Most people can enjoy a few cups a day. Cut back if you notice heartburn.
Is peppermint tea safe in pregnancy?
Peppermint tea is commonly drunk in pregnancy, but safety data for medicinal use are limited, and it can worsen pregnancy heartburn. Ask your midwife if unsure.
