Linden

Tilia cordata & T. platyphyllos

Honeyed • Floral • Mellow

The honey-scented blossom of Britain’s lime trees, known in France as tilleul. How to make linden tea, its traditional use for colds and stress, and what the evidence really shows.

Quick answer

Is linden tea good for you?

Linden tea, also called lime flower tea or tilleul, is made from the dried flowers and pale bracts of lime trees (Tilia). It has nothing to do with the citrus fruit. It is honeyed, gentle and caffeine-free. European regulators recognise its traditional use for the symptoms of colds and mild stress, but there is little good clinical research.

Side effects & who should take care

  • An occasional cup is likely safe for most adults.
  • If you have a heart condition, ask your doctor before drinking it regularly.
  • Not enough is known about safety in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • It may add to the effect of sedatives.

Linden At A Glance

What is linden?

Linden, or lime, trees are large, leafy trees common in British parks and avenues. Their fragrant summer flowers make a gentle tea.

Botanical Names Tilia cordata (small-leaved lime), T. platyphyllos (large-leaved lime)
Family Malvaceae (mallow family)
Part Used Flowers with bracts
Character Honeyed, floral
Native Range Europe, including Britain

Linden, British avenues and the evidence

Lime trees line streets and parks across Britain, and bees love their summer flowers.

01 • Know The Blossom

Not a citrus. A tree blossom.

Linden tea is made from the small, cream-coloured flowers of lime trees, together with the pale, tongue-shaped bract they hang from.

In Britain the trees are called limes, but they are not related to the citrus fruit. In America they are called basswood, and in France the tea is tilleul.

The flowers are picked in early summer and dried.

The tea is mellow and honey-scented, with a soft, slightly mucilaginous texture.

Lime tree, not lime fruit. A honeyed blossom from British avenues.

02 • Linden Benefits

What is linden tea good for?

Linden is a traditional European tea for colds and calm.

The honest picture: long traditional use, little clinical proof.

European regulators recognise linden flower as a traditional herbal medicine for relieving symptoms of the common cold and mild mental stress, based on long-standing use rather than strong trials. Linden has calming effects in animal studies, but there is no good human evidence that it treats colds, anxiety or insomnia.

10 • How To Make Linden Tea

How to make linden tea.

Linden is mild, so it needs a slightly longer steep.

Linden flower tea

Put 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried linden flowers and bracts in a cup.

Pour over 250 ml of just-boiled water.

Cover and steep for 5 to 10 minutes, then strain.

Add honey and lemon for a cold-season cup.

Step 01 • Measure 1–2 tsp dried flowers.
Step 02 • Pour 250 ml just-boiled water.
Step 03 • Steep 5–10 minutes, covered.
Step 04 • Finish Honey and lemon to taste.

11 • Linden Side Effects & Safety

Gentle, with a few cautions.

Heart, pregnancy and sedatives.

Linden tea in normal amounts is generally well tolerated, with a low risk of side effects.

Heart conditions: some health references advise people with heart problems to avoid linden. If you have a heart condition, ask your doctor before drinking it regularly.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding: there is no reliable information on safety, so avoid regular use.

Sedatives: linden might add to the effect of sleeping tablets and sedatives. Ask a pharmacist.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist or doctor before using linden regularly if you have a heart condition, take sedatives or are pregnant.

The Rootz Library is educational and does not diagnose, treat or replace medical advice.

Linden tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Is linden tea good for you?
Linden tea is a gentle, caffeine-free drink with long traditional use for colds and calm. Clinical evidence is limited.
Is linden the same as lime?
Linden comes from lime trees (Tilia), which are not related to the citrus lime.
What is lime flower tea?
Another name for linden tea, made from the flowers of lime trees.
How do you make linden tea?
Steep 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried flowers and bracts in 250 ml of just-boiled water for 5 to 10 minutes, covered.
Does linden tea help a cold?
European regulators recognise its traditional use for cold symptoms, but there is no good proof it treats colds.
Does linden tea help you sleep?
It is a traditional calming cup, but there is no good evidence it treats insomnia.
Is linden tea safe in pregnancy?
There is no reliable information on safety in pregnancy, so it is best avoided.
Is linden tea safe with heart problems?
Some references advise caution. Ask your doctor first if you have a heart condition.
What is tilleul?
Tilleul is the French name for linden tea, a popular after-dinner drink.
Does linden tea contain caffeine?
No. Linden tea is naturally caffeine-free.
