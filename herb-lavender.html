Lavender

Lavandula angustifolia

Floral • Perfumed • Slightly Bitter

The purple scent of English summer gardens. How to make lavender tea that tastes good, what the research really shows about calm, why oil and tea are different, and who should be careful.

Quick answer

Is lavender tea good for you?

Lavender tea is made from the dried flower buds of Lavandula angustifolia (English lavender). It is floral, caffeine-free and calming to make. Research on lavender for anxiety mostly uses a lavender oil capsule, not tea, and the studies are small. Lavender in food amounts is likely safe, but use only a little: too much tastes soapy.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Use culinary lavender, not garden flowers sprayed with chemicals.
  • Never drink lavender essential oil from a diffuser bottle.
  • Not enough is known about lavender by mouth in pregnancy or breastfeeding; avoid medicinal amounts.
  • It may add to the effect of sedatives; ask a pharmacist.

Lavender At A Glance

What is lavender?

Lavender is a fragrant, woody shrub in the mint family. English lavender is the type used for tea and cooking.

Botanical Name Lavandula angustifolia
Family Lamiaceae (mint family)
Part Used Flower buds
Character Floral, perfumed
Native Range Mediterranean

Lavender, calm and the evidence

Lavender is native to the Mediterranean and grows in fields from Provence to Norfolk.

It is used in food, drinks, soaps and aromatherapy.

Small studies of a lavender oil capsule suggest it might help anxiety. Lavender tea itself has very little research.

01 • Know The Flower

Purple spikes, a big scent.

Lavender is a small, woody shrub with grey-green leaves and spikes of purple flowers.

English lavender (Lavandula angustifolia) is the sweetest and best for food and tea. Other types, such as French lavender, are stronger and more camphor-like.

Only the flower buds are used for tea, and only a little: a teaspoon makes a cup.

Lavender essential oil is a concentrate for the skin and air, not a drink.

A pinch for tea. A drop for the air. Never mix the two up.

02 • Lavender Benefits

What is lavender tea good for?

Lavender is famous for calm. Most of the research is on oil, not tea.

The honest picture: a calming ritual, with little tea research.

Studies of a lavender oil product taken by mouth suggest it might help anxiety, but they are small and have limitations. It is unclear whether lavender aromatherapy helps stress, sleep or pain. Very early reports suggest lavender capsules or tea might ease low mood in some people, but this needs much more research. A lavender cup can still be a pleasant wind-down.

04 • World Heritage

From Mediterranean hills to Norfolk fields.

Lavender grows wild on dry hills around the Mediterranean, and the Romans used it to scent baths and clothes.

It spread through European monastery and cottage gardens, and England became famous for its lavender fields.

Today lavender is grown commercially in places such as Norfolk and the Cotswolds, as well as Provence in France.

10 • How To Make Lavender Tea

How to make lavender tea.

Lavender is strong. Short steeping keeps it floral, not soapy.

Lavender tea

Put 1 teaspoon of dried culinary lavender buds in a cup or tea infuser.

Pour over 250 ml of water just off the boil.

Cover and steep for 3 to 5 minutes. Longer makes it bitter and soapy.

Strain, and add a little honey or lemon. It also blends well with chamomile or mint.

Step 01 • Measure 1 tsp culinary lavender.
Step 02 • Pour 250 ml just-boiled water.
Step 03 • Steep 3–5 minutes, covered.
Step 04 • Sweeten Honey or lemon to taste.

11 • Lavender Side Effects & Safety

Lovely in a cup. Careful with oils.

Oil, pregnancy and medicines.

Lavender in the amounts used in food and tea is likely safe for most adults. Oral lavender products might cause burping, headache, nausea or diarrhoea in some people.

Essential oil: do not drink lavender essential oil sold for diffusers or skin. Aromatherapy oil can cause headaches or skin irritation in some people.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding: there is not enough evidence to know whether lavender by mouth is safe, so avoid medicinal amounts.

Medicines: lavender might add to the effect of sedatives and sleeping tablets. Ask a pharmacist.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist or doctor before using lavender supplements, especially if you take sedatives, antidepressants or other regular medicines.

12 • Questions People Ask About Lavender

Lavender tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Is lavender tea good for you?
Lavender tea is a floral, caffeine-free drink. Research on lavender for anxiety mostly uses an oil capsule, so evidence for tea is limited.
Does lavender tea help you sleep?
It is a traditional bedtime drink, but there is little research on lavender tea for sleep. A calm evening ritual can still help.
How do you make lavender tea?
Steep 1 teaspoon of dried culinary lavender buds in 250 ml of just-boiled water for 3 to 5 minutes, covered, then strain.
Why does my lavender tea taste like soap?
Too much lavender or steeping it too long. Use 1 teaspoon and steep for 3 to 5 minutes.
Can I use lavender from my garden?
Yes, if it is English lavender and has not been sprayed with chemicals. Use the buds only.
Is lavender tea safe in pregnancy?
There is not enough evidence on lavender by mouth in pregnancy, so avoid medicinal amounts and ask your midwife.
Can you drink lavender essential oil?
No. Essential oils sold for diffusers or skin are not made to be swallowed.
Does lavender tea contain caffeine?
No. Lavender tea is naturally caffeine-free.
What is culinary lavender?
Usually English lavender (Lavandula angustifolia), grown and dried for food and drinks.
Can lavender interact with medicines?
It might add to the effect of sedatives. Ask a pharmacist if you take regular medication.
