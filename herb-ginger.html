01 • Know The Root

What exactly is ginger?

Ginger is a perennial rhizomatous plant in the Zingiberaceae family. The familiar “ginger root” used in food and drinks is botanically an underground stem called a rhizome.

Fresh ginger has a bright, pungent and aromatic character. Drying alters that aromatic profile and produces a different, often sharper expression.

Royal Botanic Gardens, Kew records Zingiber officinale as an accepted species with a native range from the Eastern Himalaya to south-central China.

A plant can begin in one part of the world and become part of another culture's everyday life.

03 • African & Caribbean Heritage

A travelled root. A local tradition.

Ginger did not originate in Africa or the Caribbean. Its botanical origins lie in Asia, but centuries of cultivation, commerce and cultural exchange carried ginger far beyond its native range.

Ginger became established in tropical growing regions around the world, including Africa and the Caribbean. Jamaica developed a particularly strong historical association with ginger cultivation.

Across Caribbean communities, ginger is familiar in teas, juices, cooking, seasonal drinks and household preparations. It is now part of living Caribbean food culture rather than simply a plant imported from elsewhere.

Asian Origins

Kew records the native range of Zingiber officinale from the Eastern Himalaya to south-central China.

Tropical Africa

Ginger became cultivated in tropical African regions and incorporated into local culinary and plant traditions.

Caribbean

Ginger became part of Caribbean food, drink and household traditions.

Jamaica

Jamaica became particularly associated with cultivated ginger and strongly aromatic ginger products.

05 • Ginger Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

This graphic does not show organs that ginger “treats.” It highlights systems and outcomes that researchers have investigated.

Research Area 01

Nausea & digestive research

Nausea is one of the most frequently investigated clinical areas for ginger. Findings differ depending on the type of nausea, preparation and dose.

Research Area 02

Muscles, joints & discomfort

Ginger has been investigated in research involving pain, muscle soreness and osteoarthritis-related outcomes.

Research Area 03

Metabolic & inflammatory markers

Researchers have investigated ginger in relation to glucose, lipids and inflammatory biomarkers. Evidence varies by outcome.

06 • What Modern Research Says

Evidence, not exaggeration.

Ginger has been investigated across many health outcomes. Evidence strength varies, and results from supplements or extracts cannot automatically be transferred to a normal cup of herbal tea.

Better Studied

Pregnancy-related nausea

Research suggests ginger may help reduce pregnancy-related nausea in some settings. Pregnancy still warrants individual healthcare guidance, particularly when concentrated supplements are involved.

Mixed / Context Dependent

Other forms of nausea

Ginger has also been investigated for postoperative, chemotherapy-related and motion-related nausea. Findings are not consistently positive.

Emerging / Variable

Metabolic & inflammatory outcomes

Studies have examined glucose, lipids, discomfort and inflammatory markers. Evidence quality and confidence differ considerably between outcomes.

Better studied

Multiple human studies or reviews investigate the specific outcome.

Mixed / context dependent

Human research exists, but results vary between studies, preparations or populations.

Emerging / variable

An active research area where evidence is not yet strong enough for firm conclusions.

13 • Ginger Safety & Side Effects

Natural does not mean consequence-free.

Ginger is food — but form and dose matter.

Ginger used as an ordinary food or drink is not the same as concentrated extracts or dietary supplements used in clinical studies.

NCCIH notes that oral ginger may cause abdominal discomfort, heartburn, diarrhoea, and mouth or throat irritation in some people.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak with an appropriate healthcare professional before using concentrated herbal supplements.

The RastaRoots Herbal Library is educational. It does not diagnose, treat or replace individual medical advice.
