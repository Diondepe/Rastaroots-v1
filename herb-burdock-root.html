Quick answer

What is burdock root good for?

Burdock root (Arctium lappa) is a long, earthy root eaten as a vegetable in Japan (gobo) and famous in Britain’s dandelion and burdock drink. It has been used for skin, digestion and as a “blood purifier”, but there is insufficient human evidence for any of these uses.

Side effects & who should take care

  • People allergic to ragweed, daisies or chrysanthemums may react.
  • Avoid it in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • It may lower blood sugar and act as a mild diuretic.
  • Buy from a trusted supplier: it has been confused with poisonous roots.

Burdock Root At A Glance

What is burdock?

Burdock is a large plant in the daisy family, with huge leaves and sticky burrs. Its long taproot is eaten and brewed.

Botanical Name Arctium lappa
Family Asteraceae (daisy family)
Part Used Root
Character Earthy, sweet
Native Range Europe and Asia

Burdock, Britain and the evidence

Burdock grows wild across Britain, Europe and Asia.

Its root is a vegetable in Japan and a classic flavour in British dandelion and burdock.

There is not enough human evidence for its health claims.

01 • Know The Root

Big leaves, sticky burrs, long root.

Burdock has enormous leaves and purple thistle-like flowers that become hooked burrs, which inspired Velcro.

Its taproot can grow very long and is crunchy and slightly sweet.

The root contains inulin, a type of fibre.

Modern dandelion and burdock drinks usually contain flavourings, not much real root.

The burr that inspired Velcro, the root that flavours a British classic.

02 • Burdock Benefits

What is burdock root good for?

Burdock is promoted for skin, digestion and blood sugar.

The honest picture: a nourishing vegetable, unproven medicine.

Laboratory and animal studies have explored burdock for infections, inflammation and blood sugar, but there is currently insufficient human evidence for any use. It is a fibre-rich root vegetable, and that is its most reliable benefit.

10 • How To Make Burdock Root Tea

How to make burdock root tea.

Simmer the root for a fuller flavour.

Burdock root tea

Add 1 teaspoon of dried, chopped burdock root to a small pan with 300 ml of water.

Simmer gently for 10 minutes.

Strain into a cup.

Try it with an equal amount of roasted dandelion root for a classic pairing.

11 • Burdock Root Side Effects & Safety

Safe as food, with a few cautions.

Allergies, pregnancy, blood sugar and sourcing.

Burdock root eaten as a vegetable is generally safe for most people.

Allergies: people allergic to ragweed, daisies, marigolds or chrysanthemums may react.

Pregnancy and blood sugar: avoid it in pregnancy and breastfeeding. It may lower blood sugar and act as a mild diuretic, so take care with diabetes medicines and water tablets.

Sourcing: burdock root has been confused with poisonous deadly nightshade root. Buy from a trusted supplier.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist before using burdock with regular medicines.

The Rootz Library is educational and does not diagnose, treat or replace medical advice.
