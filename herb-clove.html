Clove

Syzygium aromaticum

Warm • Pungent • Sweet-Spicy

The little nail-shaped spice of Christmas sorrel and black cake. How to make clove tea and clove water, what the research shows, why clove oil is a different story, and cloves’ journey from Zanzibar to Grenada.

Quick answer

Is clove tea good for you?

Clove tea (or clove water) is made by steeping whole dried cloves, the flower buds of Syzygium aromaticum, in hot water. It is warming, fragrant and caffeine-free. Cloves contain eugenol, which is studied in the lab, but there is little human evidence for clove tea benefits. Cloves in food and tea are fine, but clove oil is a different strength: it is unsafe to swallow, especially for children.

Side effects & who should take care

  • A few cloves in tea or food are fine for most adults.
  • Never give clove oil to children by mouth: small amounts have caused seizures and liver damage.
  • Ask a pharmacist first if you have a bleeding disorder or take blood thinners.
  • Avoid clove supplements in pregnancy and breastfeeding.

Clove At A Glance

What are cloves?

Cloves are the dried, unopened flower buds of an evergreen tree in the myrtle family. Their name comes from the French clou, meaning nail.

Botanical Name Syzygium aromaticum
Family Myrtaceae (myrtle family)
Part Used Dried flower buds
Character Warm, pungent, numbing
Native Range Maluku Islands, Indonesia

Cloves, Africa, the Caribbean and the evidence

Cloves are native to the Maluku Islands of Indonesia, the original Spice Islands.

Zanzibar and Pemba in Tanzania, Madagascar and the Comoros became major clove growers, and Grenada still grows a small crop.

Cloves flavour Caribbean sorrel, Christmas ham and black cake. Health claims for clove tea are not proven.

01 • Know The Bud

A flower bud, dried into a spice.

Cloves are picked as pink flower buds, before they open, then dried in the sun until they turn dark brown and hard.

Their warm, numbing flavour comes mainly from eugenol, an aromatic oil.

Whole cloves are used in tea, mulled drinks, rice dishes, curries, sorrel and the classic studded Christmas ham.

Clove oil is a concentrated essential oil. It is used in tiny amounts on teeth, but it is not a drink.

A spice in your tea. A strong oil in a bottle. Know the difference.

02 • Clove Benefits

What is clove tea good for?

Clove water is popular online. The research behind it is mostly in the lab.

The honest picture: a warming spice tea, not a remedy.

Eugenol from cloves has been studied in laboratories for antioxidant and antibacterial activity, and clove oil is sold in the UK for temporary relief of toothache. There is little good human research on drinking clove tea or clove water for blood sugar, digestion or weight. Enjoy it as a warming, caffeine-free drink.

04 • African & Caribbean Heritage

From the Spice Islands to Zanzibar and Grenada.

Cloves are native to the Maluku Islands of Indonesia, the original Spice Islands, and were traded across Asia, Africa and Europe for centuries.

In the 1800s, cloves were planted on Zanzibar and Pemba, off the coast of Tanzania, which became world-famous clove islands. Madagascar and the Comoros are also major growers today. That history includes the forced labour of enslaved Africans on the clove plantations.

Cloves reached the Caribbean too. Grenada, the Spice Isle, still grows a small crop alongside nutmeg and cinnamon, and cloves flavour sorrel, black cake and Christmas ham across the islands.

10 • How To Make Clove Tea

How to make clove tea and clove water.

A few cloves go a long way. Start small: the flavour is strong.

Clove tea

Lightly crush 3 to 4 whole cloves.

Put them in a cup and pour over 250 ml of just-boiled water.

Cover and steep for 5 to 10 minutes, then strain.

Clove water: soak 3 to 4 whole cloves in a glass of cold water overnight, then strain and drink. Use whole cloves, never clove oil.

Step 01 • Crush 3–4 whole cloves.
Step 02 • Pour 250 ml just-boiled water.
Step 03 • Steep 5–10 minutes, covered.
Step 04 • Strain Add honey or cinnamon if you like.

11 • Clove Side Effects & Safety

Safe in your cup. Serious in a bottle.

Clove oil, children and bleeding.

Cloves used in food and tea are likely safe for most people.

Clove oil and children: clove oil is likely unsafe to take by mouth for children. Even small amounts have caused seizures, liver damage and fluid imbalances. Keep it out of reach, and call 111 or 999 if a child swallows it.

Bleeding: eugenol may slow blood clotting. Ask a pharmacist before using cloves medicinally if you have a bleeding disorder, take blood thinners or have surgery planned.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding: cloves in food are fine, but there is not enough evidence on medicinal amounts or supplements, so avoid them.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist or doctor before using clove supplements or clove oil, and never use clove oil as a drink.

12 • Questions People Ask About Clove

Clove tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Is clove tea good for you?
Clove tea is a warming, caffeine-free drink. Cloves contain eugenol, studied in the lab, but there is little human evidence for clove tea benefits.
What is clove water?
Clove water is made by soaking a few whole cloves in cold water overnight, then straining. It is a trend online with little research behind it.
How do you make clove tea?
Lightly crush 3 to 4 whole cloves, steep in 250 ml of just-boiled water for 5 to 10 minutes, covered, then strain.
Is clove oil safe to drink?
No. Clove oil is a concentrate and is likely unsafe by mouth, especially for children. Use whole cloves for tea.
Are cloves safe in pregnancy?
Cloves in normal food amounts are fine. Avoid clove supplements and oil during pregnancy and breastfeeding.
Does clove tea help toothache?
Clove oil is sold in the UK for temporary relief of toothache, applied to the tooth. Clove tea is not a treatment. See a dentist.
Where do cloves come from?
Cloves are native to Indonesia’s Maluku Islands. Today they are also grown in Madagascar, Zanzibar and Pemba in Tanzania, the Comoros and, on a small scale, Grenada.
Why are cloves used in sorrel?
Cloves add warm, sweet spice to Caribbean sorrel, alongside ginger, pimento and cinnamon.
Does clove tea contain caffeine?
No. Clove tea is naturally caffeine-free.
How many cloves are safe a day?
A few cloves in food or tea is a normal amount. There is no set limit for food use; avoid concentrated oils and supplements.
