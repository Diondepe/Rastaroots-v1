Quick answer

Does red clover help menopause?

Red clover (Trifolium pratense) contains isoflavones, plant compounds similar to oestrogen. Studies on menopause symptoms such as hot flushes have had inconsistent results: some small studies found a benefit, but a large trial and several reviews found no significant effect. Most research uses concentrated extracts, not tea.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Avoid it with hormone-sensitive cancers such as oestrogen-receptor-positive breast cancer.
  • Avoid it in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Take care with blood thinners and hormone therapies.
  • It appears safe for most adults short-term.

Red Clover At A Glance

What is red clover?

Red clover is a common meadow plant in the pea family, with three-part leaves and round pink-purple flower heads.

Botanical Name Trifolium pratense
Family Fabaceae (pea family)
Part Used Flower heads
Character Mild, grassy
Native Range Europe, western Asia, North Africa

Red clover, menopause and the evidence

Red clover grows in meadows and verges across Britain and is grown as cattle feed.

It contains isoflavones, the same group of compounds found in soy.

Studies on menopause have mixed results, and it is not suitable for everyone.

01 • Know The Flower

A meadow flower with hormone-like compounds.

Red clover is a low plant with three oval leaflets, often marked with a pale “V”, and round pink-purple flower heads.

Like peas and beans, it is a legume and adds nitrogen to soil.

Its isoflavones (including biochanin A and formononetin) can act weakly like oestrogen in the body.

Supplements are made from concentrated extracts; tea uses the dried flower heads.

A pretty meadow flower with a powerful chemistry.

02 • Red Clover Benefits

What is red clover good for?

Red clover is mainly sold for menopause symptoms.

The honest picture: mixed results, real cautions.

Several small studies found red clover extracts helped hot flushes, but a large clinical trial and several reviews found no significant benefit. Studies on cholesterol have also been inconsistent. There is not enough evidence for other uses. Extracts have been used in studies for up to 3 years with apparent safety.

04 • World Heritage

A British meadow flower.

Red clover is native to Europe, western Asia and North Africa, and grows wild across Britain.

Farmers have sown it for centuries to feed cattle and enrich soil.

In folk medicine it was used for coughs and skin complaints, and it became popular for menopause in recent decades.

10 • How To Make Red Clover Tea

How to make red clover tea.

A mild, grassy cup.

Red clover tea

Put 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried red clover flower heads in a cup.

Pour over 250 ml of just-boiled water.

Cover and steep for 10 minutes, then strain.

Add honey or mint if you like.

11 • Red Clover Side Effects & Safety

Check before you sip.

Hormones, pregnancy and blood thinners.

Red clover appears safe for most adults short-term. It can cause rash, headache, nausea or spotting.

Hormone-sensitive conditions: because its isoflavones act like oestrogen, people with oestrogen-receptor-positive breast cancer or other hormone-sensitive cancers or conditions should avoid it.

Pregnancy and breastfeeding: avoid it.

Medicines: take care with blood thinners, HRT, the contraceptive pill and tamoxifen. Ask a pharmacist.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to your GP about menopause symptoms, and to a pharmacist before using red clover with any medicines.
