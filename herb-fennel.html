Fennel

Foeniculum vulgare

Sweet • Aniseed • Warm

The sweet, aniseed-scented seed of after-dinner tea. What fennel tea is good for, how to make it properly, what the evidence shows and why young children should not have it.

Quick answer

What is fennel tea good for?

Fennel tea is made from the crushed seeds (fruits) of Foeniculum vulgare, a Mediterranean plant in the carrot family. It tastes sweet and aniseed-like. The European Medicines Agency lists fennel fruit for traditional use to ease mild digestive complaints such as bloating and wind, and bitter fennel for mild period cramps, based on long use rather than strong clinical trials.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Fennel is not recommended for children under 4 as a medicine, because of a natural compound called estragole.
  • Avoid regular medicinal use in pregnancy and breastfeeding.
  • Avoid it if you are allergic to celery, carrot or related plants.
  • Never give fennel oil to children.

Fennel At A Glance

What is fennel?

Fennel is a tall, feathery herb. The “seeds” used for tea are botanically the dried fruits.

Botanical Name Foeniculum vulgare
Family Apiaceae (carrot family)
Part Used Seed (fruit)
Character Sweet, aniseed, warm
Native Range Mediterranean

Fennel, digestion and the evidence

Fennel seed tea is one of Europe’s oldest after-dinner drinks, and fennel seeds are chewed after meals across India.

Its sweet, aniseed taste comes from anethole, an aromatic oil in the seeds.

The digestive uses are traditional and widely accepted, but strong clinical evidence is limited.

01 • Know The Seed

A sweet seed from a feathery Mediterranean herb.

Fennel grows up to two metres tall, with fine, feathery leaves and flat clusters of small yellow flowers.

The striped, green-brown seeds are actually dried fruits. There are two main types: sweet fennel and bitter fennel.

The seeds contain aromatic oils, mainly anethole, which gives fennel its liquorice-like sweetness.

They also contain small amounts of estragole, which is why fennel medicines are not recommended for young children.

Sweet seed. Aniseed warmth. The original after-dinner tea.

02 • Fennel Benefits

What is fennel tea good for?

Fennel is one of the better-established traditional digestive herbs in Europe, though clinical evidence is still limited.

The honest picture: accepted traditional use for mild bloating.

The European Medicines Agency lists fennel fruit for traditional use in mild digestive complaints such as bloating and wind, and bitter fennel for minor spasm associated with menstrual periods. These listings rely on long, safe use. Good-quality trials of fennel tea itself are few.

Regulatory Review

Bloating and wind

The EMA lists fennel fruit for traditional relief of mild digestive complaints, including bloating and flatulence.

Regulatory Review

Mild period cramps

Bitter fennel fruit is also listed for traditional relief of minor spasm associated with periods.

Evidence Context

Few strong trials

Most research is small or uses extracts and oils, not tea.

04 • Plant Heritage

From the Mediterranean to kitchens worldwide.

Fennel is native to the Mediterranean, where it grows wild on dry, sunny coasts.

The ancient Greeks called it marathon, and the Romans used it in cooking and medicine. It spread through Europe and along trade routes to India and China.

Today fennel seed is part of Indian, Middle Eastern, Mediterranean and British cooking.

07 • What Modern Research Says

Trusted tradition. Limited trials.

Fennel is well accepted as a traditional digestive herb, with careful limits for children.

Regulatory Review

EU herbal monographs

The EMA has published monographs for both sweet and bitter fennel fruit, including a tea of 1.5 g in 250 ml of water.

Key Limitation

Small studies

Clinical studies are mostly small, short or use extracts and oils rather than tea.

Safety Note

Estragole

Fennel contains estragole. The EMA advises against fennel medicines in children under 4 and in pregnancy.

10 • How To Make Fennel Tea

How to make fennel tea.

Crushing the seeds first releases the flavour. A covered cup keeps the aromatic oils in.

Fennel seed tea

Lightly crush 1 to 2 teaspoons of fennel seeds (about 1.5 to 2.5 g) with a pestle and mortar or the back of a spoon.

Put them in a mug and pour over 250 ml of freshly boiled water.

Cover and steep for 10 to 15 minutes.

Strain and drink warm after meals. Adults usually have up to 3 cups a day for short periods.

11 • Fennel Side Effects & Safety

Gentle for adults. Not for young children.

Mind the estragole.

Fennel tea is generally well tolerated by adults in normal amounts.

Fennel naturally contains estragole. Because of this, the EMA does not recommend fennel fruit medicines for children under 4, and advises against fennel oil for anyone under 18.

There is not enough data to recommend medicinal fennel in pregnancy or breastfeeding. Occasional food use is fine.

Avoid fennel if you are allergic to celery, carrot, mugwort or other plants in the carrot family.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist or doctor before giving fennel to a child, using it during pregnancy or breastfeeding, or if bloating is new, severe or lasts more than 2 weeks.

The Rootz Library is educational and does not diagnose, treat or replace medical advice.

12 • Questions People Ask About Fennel

Fennel tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

What is fennel tea good for?
Traditionally, mild digestive complaints such as bloating and wind. The European Medicines Agency accepts this traditional use. Bitter fennel is also used for mild period cramps.
Is fennel tea good for you?
For most adults, fennel tea is a gentle, caffeine-free drink. It is not recommended as a medicine for young children or in pregnancy.
How do you make fennel seed tea?
Lightly crush 1 to 2 teaspoons of fennel seeds, pour over 250 ml of boiling water, cover and steep for 10 to 15 minutes, then strain.
Does fennel tea help with bloating?
It is a traditional remedy for bloating and wind, and is listed by the EMA for that use, though strong trials are limited.
Can I drink fennel tea every day?
Most adults can drink fennel tea regularly in normal amounts. For medicinal use, the EMA suggests short courses and seeing a doctor if symptoms last more than 2 weeks.
Is fennel tea safe in pregnancy?
There is not enough data to recommend fennel as a medicine in pregnancy because of estragole. Ask your midwife before drinking it often.
Can babies have fennel tea?
Fennel medicines are not recommended for children under 4 because of estragole. Speak to your GP or health visitor about colic.
Is fennel tea good for periods?
Bitter fennel is listed by the EMA for traditional relief of minor menstrual cramps. See a doctor if period pain is severe.
What does fennel tea taste like?
Sweet and warming, with a gentle aniseed or liquorice flavour.
Does fennel tea contain caffeine?
No. Fennel tea is naturally caffeine-free.
