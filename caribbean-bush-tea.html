Understanding the tradition

What is Caribbean bush tea?

“Bush tea” is a broad expression used in parts of the Caribbean for infusions prepared from plants such as leaves, flowers, stems and roots. It does not describe one standardised ingredient list, one island-wide practice or one method of preparation.

In Jamaica and across the Caribbean, families have passed on their own ways of identifying plants, making infusions and understanding their place in everyday life. The plants and meanings can differ between communities and households.

At RastaRoots, we explore this heritage alongside botanical identity, flavour, preparation and modern research. We distinguish traditional uses from scientifically established effects.

Important: Traditional use is part of cultural history, not proof that a plant or tea treats a medical condition. Some herbs can interact with medicines or be unsuitable during pregnancy.

Quick answers

Caribbean bush tea FAQs

Is Jamaican bush tea one specific recipe?

No. It refers to a range of plant infusions; ingredients and methods vary across households and communities.

Are cerasee and bitter melon the same plant?

Both names can refer to Momordica charantia, but may describe different plant parts and preparations.

Does bush tea contain caffeine?

Infusions made only from caffeine-free botanicals generally do not contain caffeine, but recipes vary. Check ingredients, especially if a blend includes tea leaves or other caffeine-containing plants.

Can Caribbean herbal teas treat illnesses?

Traditional use does not establish medical efficacy. Herbal teas are not substitutes for medical care, and some plants have safety considerations or drug interactions.

Are RastaRoots teas grown in Jamaica?

No such claim is made. RastaRoots is a Manchester-built brand with Caribbean-inspired blends using ingredients sourced worldwide.
