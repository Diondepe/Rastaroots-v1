Lemon Balm

Melissa officinalis

Lemony • Fresh • Soft

A lemon-scented member of the mint family, grown in monastery and cottage gardens for centuries. What lemon balm is, what research really says and how to make the tea.

Explore Lemon Balm ↓ Join The Library

Quick answer

What is lemon balm good for?

Lemon balm (Melissa officinalis) is a lemon-scented herb in the mint family. It is traditionally drunk as a tea to ease mild stress, help with sleep and settle mild digestive upsets such as bloating, and European regulators accept these traditional uses. Small clinical trials suggest it may reduce anxiety, but they are few and varied, so the evidence is promising rather than proven.

Side effects & who should take care

  • It may make some people drowsy; take care with sleeping tablets, sedatives or alcohol.
  • Lab studies suggest it may affect thyroid hormone activity; ask first if you have a thyroid condition.
  • EU herbal medicines containing lemon balm are for adults and children over 12.
  • There is not enough safety data for medicinal use in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

Lemon Balm At A Glance

What is lemon balm?

Lemon balm is a bushy perennial herb in the mint family, Lamiaceae. Its leaves smell of lemon when crushed and are used fresh or dried for tea.

Botanical Name Melissa officinalis
Family Lamiaceae (mint family)
Part Used Leaves
Character Soft, lemony, calming
Native Range S. Europe, Mediterranean, W. Asia

Lemon balm, lemon balm tea and the evidence

Lemon balm is one of the easiest herbs to grow in a British garden, and one of the most searched-for herbal teas.

People mostly ask what it is, what it is good for and how to make lemon balm tea.

The European Medicines Agency accepts lemon balm’s long traditional use for mild stress, sleep and mild digestive complaints. That is a recognition of tradition, not proof from large trials.

01 • Know The Leaf

A mint without the menthol. A lemon without the lemon.

Melissa officinalis grows as a bushy clump with square stems and bright green, heart-shaped, crinkled leaves, like a softer, rounder mint.

Crush a leaf and it smells clearly of lemon. That scent comes from essential oils including citral and citronellal.

The name Melissa comes from the Greek word for honeybee. Bees love its small white summer flowers.

It is not related to lemongrass or lemon verbena, though all three are used for lemony teas.

Same family as mint. Lemon in the leaf. Bees in the flowers.

02 • Lemon Balm Benefits

What is lemon balm good for?

Lemon balm has a long tradition as a calming herb. It is one of the few herbal teas with both a formal European assessment and a set of small clinical trials.

The key distinction: traditional use is accepted; clinical proof is still limited.

European regulators accept lemon balm for mild stress, sleep and mild digestive complaints based on long-standing use. Clinical trials, mostly using capsules or extracts, look promising for anxiety but are small and varied.

Accepted Traditional Use

Mild stress and sleep

The European Medicines Agency’s herbal committee concluded lemon balm can be used for mild symptoms of stress and to aid sleep, based on long-standing use.

Human Research

Anxiety and mood

A 2021 meta-analysis of randomised trials found lemon balm improved anxiety and depression scores versus placebo, but warned the trials were few and varied.

Accepted Traditional Use

Bloating and wind

Lemon balm is also traditionally used for mild digestive complaints such as bloating and flatulence.

04 • European & Garden Heritage

From Mediterranean hillsides to British back gardens.

Lemon balm is native to southern Europe, the Mediterranean and western Asia, and has been grown for more than 2,000 years.

Greek and Roman writers described it, medieval monasteries grew it, and it became a staple of cottage gardens across Britain.

It spreads easily and grows almost anywhere, which is why so many people search for what to do with the lemon balm already in their garden.

06 • Lemon Balm Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

These are areas researchers have studied, mostly with capsules or extracts. They do not mean lemon balm tea treats these conditions.

Research Area 01

Anxiety and stress

Randomised trials have measured anxiety scores with lemon balm. Results are promising but trials are small.

Research Area 02

Sleep quality

Some studies report better sleep quality, sometimes combined with other herbs such as valerian.

Research Area 03

Mood and memory

Small studies have looked at mood and short-term memory. Evidence is early.

07 • What Modern Research Says

Promising trials. Not yet firm proof.

Lemon balm sits in a middle ground: more human research than most herbal teas, but not enough for firm conclusions.

Meta-analysis

Anxiety and depression scores

A 2021 meta-analysis found significant improvements versus placebo, without serious side effects, but called for higher-quality trials.

Regulatory Review

Traditional use accepted

The EMA herbal committee accepts lemon balm for mild stress, sleep and mild digestive complaints based on traditional use, not proven efficacy.

Key Limitation

Tea ≠ capsule

Most trials used concentrated extracts. A cup of lemon balm tea is likely to be milder.

10 • How To Make Lemon Balm Tea

How to make lemon balm tea.

One of the simplest herbal teas to make, fresh from the garden or from dried leaves.

Fresh or dried lemon balm tea

Use a small handful (about 1 to 2 tablespoons) of fresh leaves, lightly crushed, or 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried leaf per cup.

Pour over about 250 ml of water just off the boil.

Cover and steep for 5 to 10 minutes. Covering keeps the lemony oils in the cup.

Strain and drink plain, or with honey. It also works chilled as an iced tea.

Step 01 • Pick 1–2 tablespoons of fresh leaves, or 1–2 teaspoons dried.
Step 02 • Crush Lightly bruise fresh leaves to release the scent.
Step 03 • Steep Cover with 250 ml of hot water for 5–10 minutes.
Step 04 • Strain Strain and enjoy hot, or chill for iced tea.

11 • Lemon Balm Side Effects & Safety

Gentle, but still a herb.

Drowsiness, thyroid and medicines.

Lemon balm is generally well tolerated, and the European assessment recorded no reported side effects for its herbal medicines.

It may cause drowsiness in some people. Take care if you drive, or if you use sleeping tablets, sedatives or alcohol.

Laboratory studies suggest lemon balm extracts may interact with thyroid hormone activity. If you have a thyroid condition or take thyroid medication, check first.

EU herbal medicines containing lemon balm are intended for adults and children over 12. See a doctor if stress or sleep problems last more than two weeks.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist or doctor before using lemon balm medicinally. There is not enough safety data for medicinal use in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

12 • Questions People Ask About Lemon Balm

Lemon balm questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

What is lemon balm?
Lemon balm is Melissa officinalis, a lemon-scented perennial herb in the mint family. Its leaves are used fresh or dried for tea and cooking.
What is lemon balm good for?
It is traditionally used for mild stress, to aid sleep and for mild digestive complaints such as bloating. European regulators accept these traditional uses, and small trials suggest it may help anxiety, but evidence is limited.
How do you make lemon balm tea?
Steep 1 to 2 tablespoons of fresh leaves, or 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried leaf, in about 250 ml of just-boiled water for 5 to 10 minutes, covered. Strain and drink.
What is lemon balm tea good for?
Lemon balm tea is traditionally drunk to relax, wind down before bed and settle the stomach after meals.
Can you eat lemon balm?
Yes. The fresh leaves can be chopped into salads, fruit, drinks and desserts for a gentle lemon flavour.
Does lemon balm help with sleep?
It is traditionally used as a sleep aid, and some small studies report better sleep quality, often combined with other herbs. It is not a treatment for insomnia.
Can lemon balm help with weight loss?
There is no good evidence that lemon balm causes weight loss.
What are the side effects of lemon balm?
It is generally well tolerated. Some people feel drowsy. People with thyroid conditions, and those taking sedatives, should check with a pharmacist first.
Is lemon balm the same as lemongrass?
No. Lemon balm is a mint-family herb (Melissa officinalis). Lemongrass is a tropical grass (Cymbopogon citratus), known in the Caribbean as fever grass.
Where can I buy lemon balm?
Lemon balm plants are sold in most UK garden centres. Dried lemon balm tea is sold in health food shops, supermarkets and online.
