Know Your Rootz

What is the RastaRoots Herbal Library?

The RastaRoots Herbal Library is an educational collection built around the plants used throughout RastaRoots and the wider herbal traditions that surround them.

Each botanical entry separates plant identity, flavour, preparation, heritage, traditional context and modern research rather than reducing an herb to a list of internet “benefits”.

Caribbean and African plant knowledge matters here. Traditional knowledge is respected as traditional knowledge, while modern research is presented as modern research. They are connected without pretending they are the same thing.

The Herbal Library is educational. It does not diagnose, treat or replace professional medical advice.

Caribbean Herbal Tea Explained

What is Caribbean bush tea?

Caribbean bush tea is a broad term for herbal infusions made from leaves, roots, flowers and other plant parts. Ingredients and methods differ between islands, households and communities. Familiar examples include ginger, lemongrass, mint, cerasee and sorrel (hibiscus).

These traditions are part of a wider cultural history. A plant's traditional reputation is not the same as proof that a tea prevents or treats illness.

What is Jamaican bush tea?

Jamaican bush tea refers to herbal infusions made from locally familiar plants, including cerasee, ginger and lemongrass. The plants and preparation vary between households; bush tea is not a single standard recipe.

Which herbs are used in Caribbean bush tea?

Common examples include ginger, lemongrass, mint, cerasee and hibiscus (often called sorrel). Other plants are used across different Caribbean communities. Learn each plant’s identity and preparation before using it.

Is cerasee the same plant as bitter melon?

Cerasee and bitter melon can both refer to Momordica charantia. In Caribbean bush-tea contexts, cerasee commonly refers to the vine or leaves, while bitter melon often refers to the fruit. Preparations and safety considerations differ.

What is sorrel tea made from?

Caribbean sorrel drinks are commonly prepared from the fleshy calyces of Hibiscus sabdariffa, often with spices. Recipes vary and can be served hot or chilled. Explore the hibiscus guide for plant details.

What is sarsaparilla traditionally used for?

Sarsaparilla has a history in Caribbean roots drinks and other traditional preparations. Traditional uses for skin and joint concerns are not proof of clinical effectiveness; species, plant parts and product formulations matter.

Does ginger herbal tea contain caffeine?

An infusion made only from ginger and other naturally caffeine-free herbs generally contains no caffeine. Always check the ingredient list for added black tea, green tea or other caffeine sources.

How do you prepare Caribbean herbal infusions?

Preparation depends on the plant part. Leaves and flowers are often steeped, while some roots require different methods. Identify the plant accurately and follow product instructions rather than assuming one brewing method suits every herb.

Can herbal teas interact with medication or be unsuitable during pregnancy?

Yes. Some herbs can interact with medicines or be unsuitable during pregnancy, breastfeeding or certain health conditions. Consult a qualified healthcare professional before using medicinal-strength preparations or unfamiliar herbs.
