Rooibos

Aspalathus linearis

Sweet • Earthy • Vanilla

South Africa’s red bush tea, grown in one small mountain region. What rooibos is, why it has no caffeine, what research really says and how to brew it.

Quick answer

What is rooibos tea, and is it good for you?

Rooibos (say “ROY-boss”), also called red bush tea, is made from Aspalathus linearis, a shrub from South Africa’s Cederberg mountains. It is naturally caffeine-free, low in tannins and tastes sweet, earthy and slightly vanilla. It contains a rare antioxidant called aspalathin. Human trials are few, so specific health claims are unproven, but it is a widely enjoyed, gentle everyday tea.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Rooibos is generally well tolerated as an everyday tea.
  • A few case reports have linked very heavy use or extracts to liver problems; if you have liver disease, check first.
  • Concentrated extracts are not the same as a cup of tea.
  • There is limited research in pregnancy; ordinary cups are widely drunk, but avoid extracts.

Rooibos At A Glance

What is rooibos?

Rooibos is a shrub in the legume family, Fabaceae, with fine, needle-like leaves. The name is Afrikaans for “red bush”.

Botanical Name Aspalathus linearis
Family Fabaceae (legumes)
Parts Used Leaves & fine stems
Character Sweet, earthy, vanilla
Native Range Cederberg, South Africa

Rooibos tea, caffeine and the evidence

Rooibos is one of the most popular caffeine-free teas in the UK. People most often ask what it is, whether it is good for you and whether it has caffeine.

It has no caffeine and is low in tannins. Its polyphenols, especially aspalathin, interest researchers, but human trials remain small.

Since 2021, “rooibos” has had protected designation of origin status in the EU, the first African food to receive it.

01 • Know The Bush

A red tea that is not tea at all.

Rooibos is not related to the tea plant, Camellia sinensis. It is a legume, a distant cousin of peas and beans.

Its leaves are thin and needle-like. After harvest they are cut, bruised and left to oxidise (“ferment”), which turns them red and develops a sweet, earthy flavour.

Green rooibos is unfermented. It tastes lighter and grassier and keeps more of its aspalathin.

Rooibos is a close neighbour of honeybush, which grows in the same fynbos region but is a different plant.

Red bush, not red tea. A legume, not a Camellia. No caffeine, ever.

02 • Rooibos Tea Benefits

Is rooibos tea good for you?

Rooibos is a gentle, caffeine-free, low-tannin tea. Research interest centres on its unusual antioxidants.

The honest picture: interesting antioxidants, limited human trials.

A scoping review of rooibos studies in people found only a small number of human trials, and called for larger clinical trials. Most claims about blood sugar, heart health and skin come from lab and animal research.

Everyday Benefit

No caffeine, low tannin

Rooibos never contains caffeine and is lower in tannins than black tea, which suits evenings and people avoiding caffeine.

Laboratory Research

Aspalathin

Rooibos is one of the only known natural sources of aspalathin, an antioxidant studied for blood sugar and heart health in the lab.

Human Research

Small trials only

A handful of small human studies have measured antioxidant and heart-health markers. Results are promising but limited.

04 • South African Heritage

One mountain region. A tea for the world.

Wild rooibos grows naturally only in a small area of the Western Cape, centred on the Cederberg mountains north of Cape Town.

Rooibos tea is linked with Khoi-descended communities of the Cederberg. Written records of the tea tradition go back to the late 19th century, and the oxidising process may have been shaped by colonial tea-drinking habits.

Commercial farming began in the 20th century. Since 2021 the name rooibos has been protected in the EU, so only rooibos from this South African region can use it.

Native Range

Cederberg region of the Western Cape.

Name

Afrikaans for “red bush”.

Protected Name

Fynbos Family

06 • Rooibos Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

These are areas researchers have studied, mostly in the lab and in animals. They do not mean rooibos tea treats these conditions.

Research Area 01

Antioxidant activity

Aspalathin and other rooibos polyphenols show antioxidant activity in laboratory studies.

Research Area 02

Heart and cholesterol markers

A small human study reported changes in lipid and oxidative stress markers in adults drinking six cups a day.

Research Area 03

Blood sugar

Aspalathin has been studied for blood sugar control in animals. Human evidence is lacking.

07 • What Modern Research Says

Promising chemistry. Few human trials.

Rooibos has attracted a lot of research interest, but most of it has not yet moved into large studies in people.

Scoping Review

Limited human trials

A scoping review of rooibos in humans found a small body of evidence and called for dose-response studies and large clinical trials.

Everyday Context

Caffeine-free, low tannin

Its clearest benefit is practical: a gentle tea with no caffeine and less tannin than black tea.

Safety Note

Rare case reports

A few case reports have linked heavy rooibos use to liver problems. These are rare and causation is unclear.

10 • How To Brew Rooibos

How to brew rooibos tea.

Rooibos is hard to over-brew. Longer steeping makes it richer, not bitter.

Simple rooibos tea

Use 1 tea bag or 1 to 2 teaspoons of loose rooibos per cup.

Pour over about 250 ml of freshly boiled water.

Steep for 5 minutes or longer. Because it is low in tannins, it will not turn bitter.

Drink plain, with milk and honey like South Africans often do, or chill for iced tea.

Step 02 • Pour About 250 ml of freshly boiled water.
Step 03 • Steep 5 minutes or longer.
Step 04 • Serve Plain, with milk, or iced.

11 • Rooibos Side Effects & Safety

Gentle and caffeine-free.

Generally well tolerated.

Rooibos is widely drunk as an everyday tea and is generally well tolerated.

A small number of case reports have linked heavy rooibos consumption or extracts to liver problems. These are rare, and it is not clear rooibos was the cause.

If you have liver disease, or take medicines that affect the liver, check with a pharmacist before drinking large amounts.

There is limited research in pregnancy. Ordinary cups are widely drunk, but concentrated extracts should be avoided.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, ask a pharmacist or doctor before using rooibos extracts or supplements. Ordinary cups of rooibos are widely enjoyed as a caffeine-free drink.

The Rootz Library is educational and does not diagnose, treat or replace medical advice.

12 • Questions People Ask About Rooibos

Rooibos questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

What is rooibos tea?
Rooibos tea is a caffeine-free herbal tea made from Aspalathus linearis, a shrub that grows in South Africa’s Cederberg region. It is also called red bush tea.
Is rooibos tea good for you?
It is a caffeine-free, low-tannin tea with interesting antioxidants. Human trials are limited, so specific health claims are unproven, but it is a gentle everyday drink for most people.
Does rooibos tea have caffeine?
No. Rooibos is naturally caffeine-free. It never contained caffeine, so it is not “decaffeinated”.
What does rooibos tea taste like?
Sweet, earthy and smooth, with notes of honey, vanilla and dried fruit. Green rooibos is lighter and grassier.
How do you pronounce rooibos?
Say “ROY-boss”. It is Afrikaans for “red bush”.
What is rooibos red tea good for?
It is mainly enjoyed as a caffeine-free alternative to black tea. Its antioxidants are studied in the lab, but benefits in people are not proven.
Does rooibos tea stain teeth?
Rooibos is lower in tannins than black tea, so it is generally thought to stain teeth less, although any coloured drink can stain over time.
Is rooibos tea a diuretic?
Rooibos is not known as a strong diuretic. Like any drink, it adds to your fluid intake.
Is rooibos the same as honeybush?
No. Both come from South Africa’s fynbos and are legumes, but rooibos is Aspalathus linearis and honeybush is Cyclopia.
Can I drink rooibos tea every day?
Most people can enjoy several cups a day. If you have liver disease, check with a pharmacist before drinking large amounts.
