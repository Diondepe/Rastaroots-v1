Liquorice Root

Glycyrrhiza glabra

Sweet • Anise-like • Earthy

The naturally sweet root behind Pontefract cakes and Egypt’s Ramadan drink, erk sous. How to make liquorice tea, what research shows and why too much can raise your blood pressure.

Quick answer

Is liquorice root tea good for you?

Liquorice root (Glycyrrhiza glabra) makes a naturally sweet, caffeine-free tea. It has a long history in European, Middle Eastern and Chinese medicine, mainly for coughs and the stomach, but good human evidence is limited. Its sweet compound glycyrrhizin can raise blood pressure and lower potassium if you have a lot of it or have it regularly over weeks.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Avoid it with high blood pressure, heart or kidney disease.
  • Avoid it in pregnancy.
  • Do not combine with water tablets (diuretics), digoxin or steroids.
  • Keep it occasional: regular daily cups add up.

Liquorice Root At A Glance

What is liquorice root?

Liquorice is a tall plant in the pea family. Its long, woody roots taste up to 50 times sweeter than sugar.

Botanical Name Glycyrrhiza glabra
Family Fabaceae (pea family)
Part Used Root
Character Very sweet, anise-like
Native Range Southern Europe, North Africa, western Asia

Liquorice, Egypt, Yorkshire and the evidence

Liquorice root has been used for thousands of years, and was found in the tomb of Tutankhamun.

In Egypt, a chilled liquorice drink called erk sous is a Ramadan favourite. In England, Pontefract in Yorkshire was famous for liquorice sweets.

Its sweetness comes from glycyrrhizin, which can raise blood pressure in large or regular amounts.

01 • Know The Root

A root sweeter than sugar.

Liquorice is a perennial plant with feathery leaves and pale purple flowers. Its long roots are dug up, dried and cut into sticks or chips.

The root contains glycyrrhizin, which is many times sweeter than sugar.

Most black liquorice sweets in the UK contain real liquorice extract, as well as aniseed for flavour.

DGL (deglycyrrhizinated liquorice) has most glycyrrhizin removed, which reduces the blood pressure risk.

Sweet as sugar. Strong as medicine. Respect the dose.

02 • Liquorice Benefits

What is liquorice root good for?

Liquorice is used for coughs, sore throats and stomach problems. Good human evidence is limited.

The honest picture: long tradition, little proof, real risks.

Liquorice has a long history in European, Middle Eastern and Chinese medicine for coughs, sore throats and stomach complaints. Some small studies have looked at DGL for indigestion and liquorice for hepatitis C, but there is not enough evidence to confirm benefits. The risks from glycyrrhizin, however, are well known.

04 • African & World Heritage

From Tutankhamun’s tomb to Yorkshire.

Liquorice grows wild from southern Europe and North Africa to western Asia. Liquorice root was found among the treasures in Tutankhamun’s tomb.

In Egypt today, street sellers pour cold erk sous from brass jugs, and it is a favourite drink to break the fast during Ramadan.

In England, monks grew liquorice at Pontefract in Yorkshire, which became famous for its liquorice sweets, the Pontefract cakes.

10 • How To Make Liquorice Root Tea

How to make liquorice root tea.

A little goes a long way. It is very sweet.

Liquorice root tea

Add 1 teaspoon (about 2 g) of dried liquorice root chips to a small pan with 300 ml of water.

Simmer gently for 5 to 10 minutes.

Strain. It needs no sugar.

Keep it to an occasional cup, not daily for weeks, and skip it if you have high blood pressure.

11 • Liquorice Root Side Effects & Safety

Enjoy occasionally. Respect the dose.

Blood pressure, potassium, pregnancy and medicines.

In small amounts, short-term, liquorice is likely safe for most people.

Blood pressure and potassium: large amounts, or smaller amounts regularly over weeks, can cause high blood pressure, water retention and low potassium, which can lead to heart problems. People with high blood pressure, heart disease or kidney disease are more sensitive and should avoid it.

Pregnancy: heavy liquorice consumption in pregnancy has been linked to early birth, so avoid liquorice tea while pregnant.

Medicines: do not combine with diuretics (water tablets), digoxin, steroids or blood thinners. DGL products have most glycyrrhizin removed and cause fewer of these effects.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, check with a pharmacist or GP before drinking liquorice tea regularly, especially if you have high blood pressure or take regular medicines.

12 • Questions People Ask About Liquorice Root

Liquorice root questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Is liquorice root tea good for you?
It is a sweet, caffeine-free tea with traditional uses, but evidence is limited and too much can raise blood pressure.
Does liquorice raise blood pressure?
Yes, in large or regular amounts. Its glycyrrhizin causes salt and water retention and potassium loss.
How much liquorice tea is safe?
An occasional cup is fine for most healthy adults. Avoid daily use over several weeks.
How do you make liquorice root tea?
Simmer 1 teaspoon of dried root in 300 ml of water for 5 to 10 minutes, then strain.
Is liquorice tea safe in pregnancy?
No. Avoid it during pregnancy.
What is DGL?
Deglycyrrhizinated liquorice, with most glycyrrhizin removed to reduce the blood pressure risk.
What is erk sous?
A chilled Egyptian liquorice drink, popular during Ramadan.
Are liquorice and aniseed the same?
No. They taste similar, but come from different plants.
Can liquorice interact with medicines?
Yes, including diuretics, digoxin, steroids and blood thinners. Ask a pharmacist.
Does liquorice tea contain caffeine?
No. Pure liquorice root tea is caffeine-free.
