Jamaican Sorrel Drink

Ruby • Tart • Ginger-Spiced

The ruby-red drink of a Caribbean Christmas. A classic Jamaican sorrel recipe with ginger and pimento, plus low-sugar, zobo and bissap variations.

Get The Recipe ↓ Herbal Library

Quick answer

How do you make Jamaican sorrel drink?

Simmer 60 g dried sorrel with fresh ginger, pimento, cloves and a cinnamon stick in 2 litres of water for 10 minutes. Cover and steep overnight. Strain, sweeten to taste, chill and serve over ice. Sorrel is the dried calyx of Hibiscus sabdariffa, and in Jamaica it is the Christmas drink.

The recipe in five steps

  1. Rinse 60 g dried sorrel.
  2. Simmer with ginger, pimento, cloves and cinnamon in 2 litres of water for 10 minutes.
  3. Cover and steep 8 hours or overnight.
  4. Strain and sweeten to taste.
  5. Chill and serve over ice.

01 • Ingredients

What you need for about 2 litres.

This is a classic Jamaican-style sorrel with plenty of ginger. Adjust the spice and sugar to your family’s taste.

01 • Sorrel

60 g dried sorrel

About 2 cups of dried hibiscus calyces (sorrel, roselle, zobo or bissap).

02 • Ginger

60 g fresh ginger

A thumb-and-a-half piece, washed and grated or thinly sliced. Use more for extra heat.

03 • Spice

Pimento, clove & cinnamon

10–12 pimento (allspice) berries, 6 cloves and 1 cinnamon stick.

04 • Water & Sugar

2 litres water + sugar to taste

Start with about 150 g sugar and add more once cold. Optional: peel of 1 orange, juice of 1 lime.

02 • Method

How to make Jamaican sorrel, step by step.

Classic Christmas sorrel

Rinse the dried sorrel in a sieve under cold water to remove any dust.

Put the sorrel, ginger, pimento, cloves, cinnamon and orange peel in a large pan with 2 litres of water. Bring to the boil, then simmer for 10 minutes.

Turn off the heat, cover and leave to steep for at least 8 hours or overnight. The longer it steeps, the deeper the colour and flavour.

Strain through a fine sieve. Stir in sugar while it is still slightly warm, or sweeten once cold, tasting as you go. Add lime juice if you like.

Chill and serve over ice. For adults, a small splash of white rum is traditional at Christmas.

03 • Variations

Make it your own.

Extra Ginger

Fiery sorrel

Double the ginger for a warming kick that balances the sweetness.

Low Sugar

Lighter sorrel

Sweeten lightly with honey, or mix half and half with apple juice.

Zobo-Style

Nigerian zobo

Add pineapple peel and extra ginger while it steeps, as many Nigerian families do.

Bissap-Style

Senegalese bissap

Skip the pimento, add fresh mint and a little vanilla once strained.

Hot Sorrel

Warm sorrel tea

Steep a teaspoon of dried sorrel with a slice of ginger in a cup of hot water for 10 minutes.

Sorrel Punch

Party sorrel

Top with sparkling water, orange slices and ice for a festive punch.

05 • Drink Sorrel Sensibly

Festive, not medicinal.

Sugar, blood pressure and pregnancy.

Sugar: traditional sorrel is very sweet. Sweeten to taste once cold, and try the low-sugar variation.

Blood pressure: human trials suggest hibiscus may lower blood pressure. If you take blood pressure medicine or have low blood pressure, check with a pharmacist before drinking it regularly.

Pregnancy: there is not enough evidence on hibiscus in pregnancy, and some NHS maternity units advise avoiding it. Ask your midwife.

Alcohol: if you add rum, label the jug and keep an alcohol-free version for children and anyone who is pregnant or driving.

Storage: keep sorrel covered in the fridge and drink within about 5 days.

06 • Questions People Ask

Sorrel questions, answered clearly.

How do you make sorrel drink?
Boil dried sorrel (hibiscus calyces) with fresh ginger, pimento (allspice), cloves and a cinnamon stick for about 10 minutes. Cover and leave to steep for at least 8 hours or overnight. Strain, sweeten to taste, chill and serve over ice.
What is sorrel drink?
Sorrel is a ruby-red Caribbean drink made from the dried calyces of the roselle plant, Hibiscus sabdariffa, spiced with ginger and pimento. In Jamaica it is the classic Christmas drink.
What is in sorrel drink?
Dried sorrel (hibiscus calyces), water, fresh ginger, pimento (allspice) berries, cloves, often cinnamon and orange peel, and sugar. Some families add lime juice, and adults may add white rum.
Is sorrel drink alcoholic?
Not on its own. Many families add a splash of white rum or wine for adults at Christmas, but sorrel is easy to make alcohol-free.
Is sorrel the same as hibiscus?
Yes. Caribbean sorrel is Hibiscus sabdariffa, also called roselle. It is not the leafy green sorrel (Rumex) used in European cooking. In Nigeria it is zobo, in Senegal bissap and in Ghana sobolo.
How long does homemade sorrel last?
Keep it covered in the fridge and drink within about 5 days. If it fizzes, smells sour or tastes off, throw it away.
Where can I buy dried sorrel in the UK?
Dried sorrel is sold in Caribbean and African grocers, many large supermarkets’ world-food aisles, and online. It may be labelled sorrel, hibiscus flowers, roselle, zobo or bissap.
Can pregnant women drink sorrel?
There is not enough evidence on hibiscus in pregnancy, and some NHS maternity units list hibiscus among herbs to avoid. If you are pregnant, ask your midwife before drinking sorrel regularly.
How much sorrel is safe to drink a day?
There is no official limit for sorrel drink. Hibiscus may lower blood pressure, so people on blood pressure medicines should check with a pharmacist. Most homemade sorrel is also high in sugar, so a glass or two is plenty.
Can I make sorrel with fresh sorrel?
Yes. Use about twice the weight of fresh calyces, remove the seed pods first, and follow the same method.
Why is sorrel a Christmas drink?
In the Caribbean, roselle plants flower as the days shorten, so the red calyces are ready to harvest around December. Sorrel became the drink of the season.
Can I make sorrel with less sugar?
Yes. Sweeten it lightly, use a little honey or a sugar alternative, or add apple or orange juice for natural sweetness. Sweeten to taste once it is cold.
