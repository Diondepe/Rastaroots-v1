Soursop

Annona muricata

Tropical • Creamy • Green

A Caribbean favourite, loved as juice and brewed from its leaves as tea. What soursop is, how it is traditionally used, what research really says and who should take care.

Quick answer

What is soursop, and what is soursop tea?

Soursop (Annona muricata) is a spiky green tropical fruit with creamy white flesh, native to the Caribbean and tropical Americas. Soursop tea is made from its dried leaves and is widely drunk in Jamaica as a calming evening tea. Lab and animal studies have looked at its compounds, but there are no good human trials, and it is not a proven cancer treatment.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Soursop leaves and fruit contain annonacin, a compound linked to a Parkinson’s-like illness among regular consumers in Guadeloupe.
  • Do not eat the seeds, and avoid strong or daily long-term leaf tea and concentrated supplements.
  • Take care with blood pressure or diabetes medicines; soursop may add to their effect.
  • Avoid medicinal use if pregnant or breastfeeding, and tell your doctor before any PET scan.

Soursop At A Glance

What is soursop?

Soursop is the fruit of Annona muricata, a small evergreen tree in the custard-apple family, Annonaceae. Its leaves are dried and brewed as soursop tea.

Botanical Name Annona muricata
Family Annonaceae
Parts Used Fruit pulp & leaves
Character Creamy fruit, green leaf tea
Native Range Caribbean & tropical Americas

Soursop, soursop tea and Caribbean tradition

Soursop is one of the Caribbean’s best-loved fruits, drunk as juice, punch and smoothies, and eaten fresh or frozen.

Its leaves have a separate life as soursop leaf tea, a gentle evening brew in many Jamaican and wider Caribbean homes.

Online, soursop is heavily promoted as a cancer cure under the name graviola. That claim is not supported by human research, and the plant has a real safety question that most sellers leave out.

01 • Know The Fruit

One tree. A fruit for the table and a leaf for the teapot.

Annona muricata is a small evergreen tree that grows to around 10 metres. It has glossy, dark green oval leaves with a distinctive scent when crushed.

The fruit is large and heart-shaped to oval, with a soft, spiny green skin. Inside is creamy white pulp, sweet and sour at once, studded with shiny black seeds.

In the kitchen, the pulp is the prize. In the herbal tradition, it is mostly the leaves that are dried and brewed.

Those are two different products. Research on concentrated leaf extracts does not tell you what a glass of soursop juice or a cup of leaf tea will do.

Same tree. Fruit for the glass. Leaf for the cup. Seeds never.

02 • Soursop Benefits

What is soursop good for?

Soursop fruit is a nutritious food. Soursop leaf tea has a long traditional reputation as a calming drink. Modern research on the plant is almost entirely laboratory and animal work.

The biggest myth: “graviola cures cancer.”

Compounds from soursop called acetogenins kill cancer cells in a dish and in some animal studies. That is a long way from a treatment. Soursop has not been tested in proper clinical trials in people with cancer, and no cancer organisation recommends it as a cure.

Food Value

A nutritious tropical fruit

Soursop pulp provides carbohydrate, fibre and vitamin C like many tropical fruits, and is mostly water. Eaten as food, it is part of a varied diet.

Traditionally Associated

An evening calming tea

In Jamaica and across the Caribbean, soursop leaf tea is widely described as a relaxing drink before bed. This is cultural knowledge, not a tested sleep effect.

Laboratory Research

Acetogenins and cells

Lab and animal studies have looked at soursop extracts for anti-inflammatory, blood sugar and anticancer activity. None of this has been confirmed in human trials.

04 • Caribbean & Diaspora Heritage

A fruit of the Americas, at home in the Caribbean.

Unlike many plants in the Rootz Library, soursop did not travel to the Caribbean. It is native to the tropical Americas and the Caribbean.

Across the islands it carries many names: soursop in Jamaica and the English-speaking Caribbean, corossol in the French Caribbean, guanábana in Spanish-speaking countries and graviola in Brazil.

Through migration, soursop juice, punch and leaf tea travelled with Caribbean families to Britain, North America and beyond. Frozen pulp and dried leaves are now sold in Caribbean shops across the UK.

Native Range

Tropical Americas and the Caribbean.

Many Names

06 • Soursop Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

These are areas researchers have studied using soursop extracts. They do not mean soursop tea treats these conditions.

Research Area 01

Anticancer activity in the lab

Acetogenins from soursop are toxic to cancer cells in laboratory studies and some animal models. No clinical trials in people have shown a benefit.

Research Area 02

Blood sugar and blood pressure

Animal studies suggest extracts may lower blood sugar and blood pressure. This is why soursop may add to some medicines.

Research Area 03

Nerve cell toxicity

The same acetogenins, especially annonacin, damage nerve cells in the lab, which is the reason for the safety concern.

07 • What Modern Research Says

Lots of lab studies. Almost no human trials.

Soursop is a good example of a plant that is heavily promoted online but barely tested in people. The best human evidence is not about benefits at all. It is about risk.

No Clinical Trials

Cancer claims are unproven

Graviola has not been evaluated in clinical trials for cancer. Cell and animal results cannot be assumed to work in people.

Human Observation

Atypical parkinsonism in Guadeloupe

Researchers in Guadeloupe found an unusually high rate of atypical parkinsonism linked to regular consumption of soursop fruit and leaf infusions.

Mechanism

Annonacin harms nerve cells

Lab and animal studies show annonacin, the main acetogenin in soursop, can damage nerve cells in brain areas affected by the illness.

08 • Three Ways To Read The Evidence

Tradition. Science. The unknown.

Rooted In Tradition

A loved Caribbean fruit and tea.

Soursop juice and leaf tea are a genuine part of Caribbean life and memory. That heritage deserves respect.

Studied By Science

Lab activity is real.

Soursop compounds are biologically active in laboratory and animal studies. That is also why caution is needed.

Not Yet Proven

Soursop does not cure cancer.

There is no human evidence that soursop, graviola capsules or leaf tea prevent, treat or cure cancer.

10 • How To Make Soursop Tea

How to make soursop leaf tea.

A simple, mild cup. This is a culinary-style method, not a medicinal dose.

Simple soursop leaf tea

Use 2 to 3 dried soursop leaves, or about 1 teaspoon of crumbled dried leaf, from a reputable food-grade supplier.

Rinse, tear the leaves into pieces and pour over about 250 ml of freshly boiled water.

Cover and steep for 5 to 10 minutes, then strain. The tea is mild and slightly green; honey or lime is optional.

Keep it as an occasional cup, not a strong daily habit.

11 • Soursop Side Effects & Safety

Natural does not mean harmless.

The annonacin question.

Soursop fruit and leaves contain annonacin, a natural compound that harms nerve cells in laboratory and animal studies.

In Guadeloupe, doctors found an unusually high rate of a Parkinson’s-like illness that did not respond to standard treatment. Studies linked it with regular, long-term consumption of soursop fruit and leaf infusions.

This does not mean an occasional glass of juice or cup of tea is dangerous. It does mean soursop is not something to drink strongly every day for years, and concentrated capsules deserve the most caution.

Never eat the seeds. Soursop may add to medicines that lower blood pressure or blood sugar, and it can affect some imaging tests such as PET scans.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a doctor or pharmacist before using soursop leaf tea medicinally or taking graviola supplements. Anyone with Parkinson’s disease or another nerve condition should avoid it.

12 • Questions People Ask About Soursop

Soursop questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

What is soursop?
Soursop is the fruit of Annona muricata, a small tropical evergreen tree native to the Caribbean and tropical Americas. The fruit has spiny green skin and creamy, sweet-sour white flesh with black seeds.
What is soursop good for?
Soursop fruit is a nutritious tropical food. Soursop leaf tea is traditionally drunk as a calming evening tea in the Caribbean. Lab and animal studies have looked at its compounds for blood sugar, inflammation and cancer, but none of these effects has been proven in people.
What is soursop tea?
Soursop tea is a herbal tea made by steeping dried or fresh soursop leaves in hot water. It is mild and slightly green in flavour and is popular in Jamaica as an evening drink.
Does soursop cure cancer?
No. Soursop, also sold as graviola, has not been shown to prevent, treat or cure cancer in people. Laboratory results have not been confirmed in clinical trials. Never use it in place of cancer treatment.
Is soursop safe?
Eating soursop fruit occasionally is generally considered safe. The concern is regular, long-term use of leaf tea, fruit products or concentrated supplements, because soursop contains annonacin, a compound linked to a Parkinson’s-like illness in Guadeloupe. Never eat the seeds.
What are the side effects of soursop?
The main concern is nerve toxicity from annonacin with heavy or long-term use. Soursop may also add to the effects of blood pressure and diabetes medicines, and can interfere with some imaging tests.
Is soursop the same as graviola?
Yes. Graviola, guanábana, corossol and guyabano are all names for soursop, Annona muricata.
Where can I buy soursop in the UK?
Fresh soursop is occasionally sold in Caribbean, African and Asian grocers. Frozen soursop pulp is easier to find, and dried soursop leaves are sold online by herbal and Caribbean suppliers.
Can I drink soursop tea every day?
It is best kept occasional. Because of the annonacin concern, strong daily soursop leaf tea over long periods is not advised.
Can you drink soursop tea when pregnant?
There is not enough safety information, so avoid medicinal soursop leaf tea and supplements during pregnancy and breastfeeding unless a healthcare professional advises otherwise.
