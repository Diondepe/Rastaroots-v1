Mullein

Verbascum thapsus

Mild • Earthy • Soft

The tall, velvet-leaved plant of British wasteland and old herbals. What mullein is, what it is used for, what research says about lungs and coughs, and how to make the tea safely.

Quick answer

What is mullein and is it good for your lungs?

Mullein (Verbascum thapsus), also called great mullein, is a tall plant with soft, woolly leaves and a spike of yellow flowers that grows wild across Britain and Europe. Its leaves and flowers are traditionally made into tea for coughs and chest complaints. The European Medicines Agency lists mullein flower for traditional use to relieve a sore throat with a dry cough or cold in adults and children over 12, based on long use rather than clinical trials. There is no good evidence that mullein heals or “cleans” the lungs.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Always strain mullein tea through a coffee filter or fine cloth; the tiny leaf hairs can irritate your throat.
  • Do not use it instead of treatment for asthma, COPD or a lasting cough.
  • See a doctor if a cough lasts more than 3 weeks or you cough up blood.
  • Do not smoke mullein. Any smoke harms the lungs.

Mullein At A Glance

What is mullein?

Mullein is a tall biennial plant. It spends its first year as a soft rosette of leaves, then sends up a flower spike up to two metres tall.

Botanical Name Verbascum thapsus
Family Scrophulariaceae (figwort family)
Part Used Leaf and flower
Character Mild, soft, slightly sweet
Native Range Europe, North Africa, Asia

Mullein, coughs and the evidence

Mullein has been used for coughs and chest complaints in European herbal medicine for centuries.

Online, it is often promoted for “lung health” and “lung detox”. Those claims go far beyond the evidence.

What the evidence supports is modest: a gentle, traditional tea for a sore throat with a dry cough.

01 • Know The Plant

The velvet giant of British roadsides.

Mullein is easy to spot. Its large grey-green leaves are covered in soft, woolly hairs, which is why it is sometimes called velvet plant or flannel leaf.

In its second summer it grows a tall, straight stem packed with small yellow flowers, giving it the old name Aaron’s rod.

The leaves and flowers both contain mucilage, a soft, slippery substance that coats the throat, along with saponins and other plant compounds.

Those same soft hairs can irritate your throat if they end up in your tea, so straining well is essential.

Soft leaves. Yellow flowers. Strain it well.

02 • Mullein Benefits

What is mullein good for?

Mullein is a traditional cough and throat herb. Online claims about “healing the lungs” are not backed by evidence.

The honest picture: traditional use for coughs, no lung-healing proof.

The European Medicines Agency concluded that mullein flower medicines can be used, on the basis of long-standing use, to relieve a sore throat with a dry cough and cold. It notes there is insufficient evidence from clinical trials, though the use is considered plausible and safe. There are no good trials showing mullein treats asthma, COPD, infections or lung damage.

Regulatory Review

Sore throat with dry cough

The EMA lists mullein flower for traditional relief of a sore throat with a dry cough and cold, for adults and over-12s.

Traditionally Associated

Coughs and chest colds

In European and North American folk medicine, mullein leaf tea was a common winter cough remedy.

Evidence Context

No “lung detox”

There is no good evidence that mullein cleans, repairs or detoxes the lungs.

04 • Plant Heritage

A wild British plant with an old reputation.

Great mullein is native to Europe, North Africa and Asia, and grows wild across the UK on dry banks, wasteland and roadsides.

Its use for coughs goes back to ancient Greek and Roman writers and continued through medieval European herbals.

European settlers took mullein to North America, where it spread widely and was also adopted into some Indigenous plant medicine traditions.

Native Range

Europe, North Africa and Asia.

UK Wild Plant

Common on dry banks and wasteland.

Old Names

Aaron’s rod, hag’s taper, velvet plant.

Spread

Naturalised across North America.

06 • Mullein Research Areas

Where researchers have looked.

These are areas researchers study. They do not mean mullein tea treats any condition.

Research Area 01

Soothing mucilage

Mullein contains mucilage, which coats and soothes the throat, similar to other demulcent herbs.

Research Area 02

Lab studies

Extracts have shown antiviral and anti-inflammatory activity in the lab, not in people.

Research Area 03

Ear drops

Small studies have tested herbal ear-drop mixes containing mullein. These are not tea.

07 • What Modern Research Says

Plausible for coughs. Unproven for lungs.

Mullein’s reputation online is far bigger than its research base.

Regulatory Review

Traditional use accepted

The EMA accepts mullein flower for traditional relief of sore throat with dry cough, based on at least 30 years of use.

Key Limitation

Few human trials

There are no good clinical trials of mullein tea for cough, asthma, COPD or infection.

Safety Record

Few side effects reported

At the time of the EMA assessment, no side effects had been reported with mullein flower medicines.

10 • How To Make Mullein Tea

How to make mullein tea.

The most important step is straining. Mullein’s tiny hairs can irritate your throat if they are not removed.

Simple strained mullein tea

Put 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried mullein leaf or flower into a teapot or jug.

Pour over 250 ml of freshly boiled water. Cover and steep for 10 to 15 minutes.

Strain through a paper coffee filter, muslin or a very fine cloth. A normal tea strainer is not fine enough.

Add honey and lemon if you like. Most adults keep to 1 to 3 cups a day for a few days while they have a cough or cold.

11 • Mullein Side Effects & Safety

Gentle, but not a lung treatment.

Strain it, and never smoke it.

Mullein tea is generally well tolerated. At the time of the EMA review, no side effects had been reported with mullein flower medicines.

Always strain it very well. The fine hairs on the leaves can irritate the mouth, throat and skin.

Do not smoke mullein, whatever social media says. Burning any plant produces smoke that harms the lungs.

Do not use mullein instead of your inhaler or prescribed treatment for asthma, COPD or any lung condition. EMA guidance is for adults and children over 12 only, and there is little safety data in pregnancy or breastfeeding.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, see a doctor if a cough lasts more than 3 weeks, you are short of breath, have chest pain or cough up blood. Speak to a pharmacist before using mullein if you are pregnant, breastfeeding or take regular medicines.

12 • Questions People Ask About Mullein

Mullein questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

What is mullein?
Mullein (Verbascum thapsus) is a tall wild plant with soft, woolly leaves and yellow flowers. Its leaves and flowers are used to make a traditional herbal tea for coughs.
What is mullein good for?
Traditionally, coughs and sore throats. The European Medicines Agency accepts mullein flower for traditional relief of a sore throat with a dry cough and cold, based on long use.
How do you use mullein for lungs?
Mullein is used as a tea for coughs and chest colds. There is no good evidence it heals or detoxes the lungs, and it is not a treatment for asthma, COPD or infections.
Does mullein work?
It may help soothe a sore throat and dry cough, which is why it has been used for so long. It has not been tested in good clinical trials.
How do you make mullein tea?
Steep 1 to 2 teaspoons of dried leaf or flower in 250 ml of boiled water for 10 to 15 minutes, then strain through a coffee filter or fine cloth to remove the tiny hairs.
How long does mullein take to work?
There is no proven timescale. If your cough is not improving after a week, or lasts more than 3 weeks, see a doctor.
Can you smoke mullein leaf?
You should not. Burning any plant creates smoke that irritates and damages the lungs.
How do you use mullein extract drops?
Follow the label of a reputable product. Drops are much more concentrated than tea, so ask a pharmacist first, especially if you take medicines.
Is mullein safe in pregnancy?
There is not enough safety information. Avoid mullein during pregnancy and breastfeeding unless your midwife or doctor advises it.
Does mullein grow in the UK?
Yes. Great mullein is a native wild plant found on dry banks, wasteland and roadsides across Britain.
