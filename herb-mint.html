Mint At A Glance

What is mint?

Mint is a broad name for plants in the Mentha genus. This Rootz Library entry focuses specifically on spearmint, Mentha spicata, a familiar aromatic herb used in food, mint tea and traditional plant culture.

Spearmint tea, mint tea benefits and Mentha spicata

Searches for mint tea benefits often combine different mint species into one category. That creates a problem: spearmint, peppermint and other mints do not have identical chemistry or research evidence.

This entry focuses on spearmint tea made from Mentha spicata. Research involving peppermint oil is therefore kept separate from research involving spearmint tea or concentrated spearmint extracts.

That distinction matters when answering questions such as “is mint tea good for you?”, “what is spearmint tea good for?” and “what are spearmint tea benefits?”.

01 • Know The Leaf

Mint is not one plant.

Mentha is a genus containing multiple species and naturally occurring hybrids. This botanical entry focuses on spearmint, Mentha spicata.

Spearmint is a perennial aromatic herb in the Lamiaceae family. Its leaves are commonly toothed, fragrant and used fresh or dried.

Mentha spicata is now cultivated and naturalised across many parts of the world, contributing to an enormous range of food and tea traditions.

Before asking what mint does, know which mint is in the cup.

02 • Mint Tea Benefits

What are the benefits of mint tea?

The answer depends on the exact mint species, preparation, concentration and outcome being studied. Spearmint tea should not automatically inherit claims from peppermint oil or concentrated mint extracts.

Rootz Library approach

“Mint tea benefits” is a broad search term. Scientifically, spearmint tea, peppermint tea, peppermint oil and spearmint extract are different preparations. We keep them separate.

Traditional Use

Digestive traditions

Mint species have long appeared in after-meal and household traditions associated with digestion and stomach comfort. Much of the stronger modern digestive research involves peppermint oil, not ordinary spearmint tea.

Limited Human Evidence

Hormonal research

Small human studies have investigated spearmint tea and androgen measurements in women. These findings are interesting, but the evidence remains limited and does not establish spearmint tea as a treatment for hormonal disorders.

Extract-Specific

Attention & cognition

Proprietary spearmint extracts have been studied in controlled human trials involving attention, memory and cognitive performance. These extracts are not equivalent to ordinary spearmint tea.

03 • Spearmint vs Peppermint

Similar family. Different mint.

Spearmint and peppermint are often confused, but they are not botanically identical and should not be treated as interchangeable when reading research.

Spearmint

Mentha spicata

Spearmint is the species this RastaRoots entry focuses on.

It is commonly used as a culinary herb and herbal tea and generally has a softer, sweeter mint character.

Human research has investigated spearmint tea in relation to androgen measurements and concentrated extracts in relation to cognition.

Peppermint

Mentha × piperita

Peppermint is a hybrid mint with its own botanical identity, chemistry and research literature.

Peppermint is generally more strongly associated with menthol and a sharper cooling profile.

Clinical research involving peppermint oil should not be used as direct evidence for the effects of spearmint tea.

Key distinction: If a study says Mentha × piperita, peppermint oil or a proprietary extract, it is not automatically evidence for a cup of Mentha spicata spearmint tea.

05 • African & Caribbean Heritage

A travelled herb. Local rituals.

Spearmint did not originate in the Caribbean. Its botanical story extends across Europe and Asia.

Through cultivation, trade, migration and household gardening, mint species spread widely and became embedded in food, tea and plant traditions far beyond their original ranges.

Across African and Caribbean communities, mint became familiar as a cultivated aromatic herb used in drinks, cooking and home gardens.

Its history demonstrates the distinction between a plant's botanical origin and the cultures that later make it part of daily life.

08 • What Modern Research Says

Species matters. Preparation matters.

There is no single “mint evidence base.” Research depends on which species, preparation, dose and population were actually studied.

Small Human Studies

Spearmint tea & androgens

Human studies have investigated spearmint tea and androgen measurements in women. Results have attracted considerable online attention, but the evidence base remains small. Spearmint tea should not be presented as a treatment for PCOS, hirsutism or other hormonal conditions.

Extract-Specific

Attention & cognition

Controlled trials have investigated proprietary spearmint extracts in relation to attention and cognitive performance. Concentrated extracts are not equivalent to ordinary spearmint tea.

Different Species

Peppermint evidence

Peppermint oil has its own clinical and safety evidence base. It should not be used as proof that Mentha spicata tea has the same effects.

11 • How To Make Mint Tea

How to make fresh spearmint tea.

Spearmint tea can be made using fresh or dried Mentha spicata leaves. The method below is a simple culinary infusion, not a medical dosage.

Simple fresh mint tea

Rinse fresh spearmint leaves and place them in a heat-safe cup or pot.

Gently bruise some of the leaves between your fingers if you want to release a stronger aroma.

Cover the leaves with freshly boiled water and allow them to infuse until the flavour reaches your preferred strength.

Dried spearmint can be prepared using the same basic infusion principle.

Step 01 • Prepare Rinse fresh spearmint or prepare clean dried leaves.
Step 02 • Bruise Gently bruise fresh leaves if you want more aroma.
Step 03 • Infuse Cover the mint with freshly boiled water.
Step 04 • Taste Allow the infusion to develop until it reaches your preferred strength.

12 • Mint Safety

Leaf, extract and essential oil are not interchangeable.

Species and preparation matter.

Culinary spearmint, ordinary spearmint tea, concentrated spearmint extracts and peppermint essential oil are different products.

Research involving a concentrated extract should not automatically be interpreted as evidence for ordinary tea.

Peppermint oil also has its own safety profile and should not be confused with spearmint leaves.

Pure spearmint tea made only from mint leaves is naturally caffeine-free. Commercial mint blends may contain caffeine if they also contain black tea, green tea or another caffeine-containing ingredient.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak with an appropriate healthcare professional before using concentrated herbal supplements or essential oils.

The Rootz Library is educational and does not diagnose, treat or replace medical advice.

13 • Questions People Ask About Mint

Mint and spearmint questions, answered clearly.

Clear answers to common searches around mint tea, spearmint tea, caffeine, hormones and peppermint.

What is mint?
Mint is the common name for plants in the Mentha genus. This RastaRoots entry focuses specifically on spearmint, Mentha spicata.
What is spearmint tea?
Spearmint tea is a herbal infusion made from fresh or dried Mentha spicata leaves. When made only from spearmint leaves, it is naturally caffeine-free.
What are spearmint tea benefits?
Spearmint tea is traditionally enjoyed as an aromatic caffeine-free herbal infusion. Small human studies have investigated hormonal outcomes, particularly androgen measurements in women. The evidence remains limited and does not establish spearmint tea as a treatment for hormonal disorders.
What are the benefits of mint tea?
Mint tea is traditionally valued as a refreshing aromatic beverage. Specific health claims depend on which mint species and preparation were actually studied. Evidence involving peppermint oil or concentrated extracts should not automatically be applied to ordinary spearmint tea.
Is mint tea good for you?
Pure mint tea can be enjoyed as part of a varied diet and is naturally caffeine-free when made only from mint leaves. Specific health effects depend on the species, preparation and evidence available.
What is spearmint tea good for?
Spearmint tea is commonly enjoyed as a refreshing herbal drink. Small human studies have explored its relationship with androgen measurements, but current evidence is not strong enough to present spearmint tea as a treatment for a medical condition.
Is spearmint the same as peppermint?
No. Spearmint is Mentha spicata. Peppermint is Mentha × piperita, a hybrid mint. Their chemistry, flavour and research evidence are not identical.
What is the difference between spearmint and peppermint?
Spearmint and peppermint are different mint taxa. Spearmint generally has a softer, sweeter mint profile, while peppermint is more strongly associated with menthol and a sharper cooling sensation. Studies on one should not automatically be used as evidence for the other.
Does spearmint tea have caffeine?
Pure spearmint tea made only from Mentha spicata leaves is naturally caffeine-free. A commercial blend may contain caffeine if black tea, green tea or another caffeine source is included.
Is mint tea caffeinated?
Pure herbal mint tea is naturally caffeine-free. Always check the ingredient list of commercial blends, because some products combine mint with caffeinated tea leaves.
How do you make mint tea?
Place fresh or dried mint leaves in a heat-safe cup or pot, cover with freshly boiled water and allow the leaves to infuse until the aroma and flavour reach your preferred strength.
How do you make spearmint tea?
Rinse fresh spearmint leaves or prepare dried spearmint. Place the leaves in a heat-safe cup or pot, cover with freshly boiled water and allow them to infuse to your preferred strength.
Can spearmint tea affect hormones?
Small human studies have investigated spearmint tea and androgen measurements in women. The findings are preliminary and do not establish spearmint tea as a treatment for PCOS, hirsutism or other hormonal disorders.
Does spearmint tea lower testosterone?
Small studies have reported changes in some androgen measurements after spearmint tea consumption in specific groups of women. The research base is too limited to conclude that spearmint tea reliably lowers testosterone across the general population.
Does spearmint tea increase estrogen?
There is not strong clinical evidence establishing that ordinary spearmint tea reliably increases estrogen in humans. Hormonal claims should not be extrapolated beyond the evidence from small studies.
Does spearmint tea help with acne?
Spearmint tea is sometimes promoted online for hormonal acne, but there is not strong clinical evidence establishing it as an acne treatment. Research into androgen measurements should not be treated as direct evidence that spearmint tea clears acne.
Does spearmint tea help with bloating?
Mint has a long traditional association with digestion, but the better-known modern gastrointestinal evidence often involves peppermint oil rather than spearmint tea. Evidence for spearmint tea specifically and bloating remains limited.
Is peppermint or spearmint tea better?
Neither is universally “better.” They are different mints with different flavour profiles, chemical compositions and evidence bases. The better choice depends on flavour preference and the reason someone wants to drink it.
Is there a best time to drink mint tea?
There is no established universal time of day when ordinary spearmint tea has been shown to provide greater general health benefit. Timing is mainly a matter of personal preference, tolerance and ritual.
