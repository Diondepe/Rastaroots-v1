Turmeric

Curcuma longa

Earthy • Warm • Peppery

The golden root of Caribbean curry pots and Indian kitchens. How to make turmeric tea, what curcumin research really shows and why supplements need more care than the spice.

Explore Turmeric ↓ Join The Library

Quick answer

Is turmeric tea good for you?

Turmeric tea is a warming drink made from the root of Curcuma longa, fresh or dried and ground. Turmeric’s best-known compound, curcumin, has been studied for joint pain, fatty liver and cholesterol, but the US National Institutes of Health says there is not enough evidence to be sure of benefits for most conditions. Curcumin is also poorly absorbed, and a cup of tea contains far less than the supplements used in trials. Enjoy it as a warming drink, not a cure.

Side effects & who should take care

  • Turmeric in food and tea amounts has a long history of safe use.
  • High-strength turmeric or curcumin supplements have been linked to rare cases of liver injury.
  • Ask a pharmacist first if you take blood thinners, diabetes medicines or have gallbladder problems.
  • Avoid supplements in pregnancy; normal cooking amounts are fine.

Turmeric At A Glance

What is turmeric?

Turmeric is the bright orange underground stem, or rhizome, of a plant in the ginger family.

Botanical Name Curcuma longa
Family Zingiberaceae (ginger family)
Part Used Rhizome (root)
Character Earthy, warm, golden
Native Range South & Southeast Asia

Turmeric, the Caribbean kitchen and the evidence

Turmeric came to the Caribbean with Indian indentured workers in the 1800s and became part of Caribbean curry, roti and rice dishes.

In Trinidad and Guyana many families still call it “saffron”, and grow it in backyard gardens.

As a tea, it is warming and earthy. The health claims around it are much bigger than the evidence.

01 • Know The Root

A golden root from the ginger family.

Turmeric grows as a leafy plant up to a metre tall. The part people use is the knobbly rhizome underground, which is bright orange inside.

Fresh turmeric looks like a small, thin ginger root. Dried and ground, it becomes the yellow powder used in curry.

Its colour comes from curcuminoids, mainly curcumin. These make up only a small part of the dried root.

Turmeric has been used in South Asian cooking, dyeing and traditional medicine for thousands of years.

Golden root. Warm spice. Big reputation, modest evidence.

02 • Turmeric Benefits

What is turmeric tea good for?

Most turmeric research uses concentrated curcumin supplements, not tea. That matters when you read health claims.

The honest picture: promising in some trials, but not proven.

The US National Center for Complementary and Integrative Health says studies have looked at turmeric and curcumin for fatty liver disease, osteoarthritis and cholesterol, but there is not enough evidence to be sure of benefits for most conditions. Curcumin is poorly absorbed, so a cup of tea delivers very little.

Clinical Research

Joints and fatty liver

Curcumin supplements have been studied for osteoarthritis pain and non-alcoholic fatty liver disease, with mixed but promising results.

Traditionally Associated

Warmth and digestion

In South Asian and Caribbean homes, turmeric is used in warming drinks and food, often for colds and digestion.

Evidence Context

Absorption matters

Curcumin is poorly absorbed. Black pepper and fat help a little, but tea is still far weaker than trial supplements.

04 • Caribbean Heritage

From India to the Caribbean, carried by indentured workers.

Turmeric is native to South and Southeast Asia, where it has been grown for thousands of years.

Between 1838 and 1917, hundreds of thousands of Indian indentured labourers travelled to Trinidad, Guyana, Jamaica and other colonies. They brought turmeric, cumin and curry cooking with them.

Today turmeric is part of Indo-Caribbean food and family life, from Diwali dishes to everyday curry, and many Trinidadians and Guyanese still call it “saffron”.

Native Range

South and Southeast Asia.

Indentureship

07 • What Modern Research Says

Lots of research. Few firm answers.

Turmeric is one of the most researched spices in the world, yet the evidence is still not strong enough for clear health claims.

Research Volume

Thousands of studies

Curcumin has been studied in labs, animals and many small human trials.

Key Limitation

Supplements, not tea

Most human studies use concentrated curcumin, often in forms designed to be better absorbed.

Safety Signal

Rare liver injury

The US LiverTox database notes rare cases of liver injury linked to turmeric supplements, especially high-absorption products.

10 • How To Make Turmeric Tea

How to make turmeric tea.

Make it with fresh root or with powder. A pinch of black pepper and a little fat help absorb curcumin.

Turmeric and ginger tea

Grate about 2 cm of fresh turmeric and 2 cm of fresh ginger into a small pan. If using powder, use ½ teaspoon of ground turmeric instead of fresh.

Add 300 ml of water and a pinch of black pepper. Bring to the boil, then simmer gently for 10 minutes.

Strain into a cup. Add a squeeze of lemon or lime and a little honey if you like.

For a creamier drink, use half water and half milk or oat milk. Turmeric stains, so wash boards and hands quickly.

Step 02 • Simmer 300 ml water and a pinch of black pepper for 10 minutes.

11 • Turmeric Side Effects & Safety

Gentle as a spice. Stronger as a supplement.

Know the difference.

Turmeric used in food and tea has a long history of safe use for most people.

High-strength turmeric and curcumin supplements are different. The US LiverTox database reports rare cases of liver injury linked to them, especially products designed to be absorbed more easily. Stop taking a supplement and see a doctor if you notice yellow skin or eyes, dark urine or pain in your upper right tummy.

Turmeric may affect blood clotting and blood sugar, and can make gallbladder problems worse.

Avoid supplements in pregnancy and while breastfeeding. Normal cooking amounts are fine.

If you are pregnant, breastfeeding, taking medication, preparing for surgery or managing a medical condition, speak to a pharmacist or doctor before taking turmeric supplements, especially if you take warfarin or other blood thinners, diabetes medicines, or have liver or gallbladder problems.

12 • Questions People Ask About Turmeric

Turmeric tea questions, answered clearly.

Straightforward answers to the questions people search for most.

Is turmeric tea good for you?
Turmeric tea is a warming, low-calorie drink. Research on curcumin is promising for some conditions but not conclusive, and tea contains much less curcumin than the supplements studied.
How do you make turmeric tea?
Simmer 2 cm of grated fresh turmeric, or ½ teaspoon of ground turmeric, in 300 ml of water with a pinch of black pepper for 10 minutes. Strain and add lemon or honey.
How do you make turmeric tea from turmeric powder?
Stir ½ teaspoon of ground turmeric and a pinch of black pepper into 300 ml of water, simmer for 5 to 10 minutes, then let it settle and pour. Powder leaves some sediment at the bottom.
Why add black pepper to turmeric tea?
Black pepper contains piperine, which helps the body absorb curcumin. Concentrated pepper-and-curcumin supplements are much stronger than tea, so ask a pharmacist before using them.
Is turmeric and ginger tea good for you?
Turmeric and ginger are both warming roots from the same plant family and are often brewed together. It makes a pleasant drink, though neither is proven to treat illness.
Can I drink turmeric tea every day?
Most people can enjoy a cup or two of turmeric tea a day. Take more care with supplements, and ask a pharmacist if you take regular medicines.
Can turmeric damage your liver?
Turmeric in food is not linked to liver damage. Rare cases of liver injury have been reported with high-strength turmeric or curcumin supplements.
Is turmeric safe in pregnancy?
Turmeric in normal cooking amounts is fine. Avoid turmeric or curcumin supplements during pregnancy and breastfeeding unless your midwife or doctor advises otherwise.
Why is turmeric called saffron in Trinidad?
In Trinidad, Guyana and parts of the Caribbean, turmeric is commonly called “saffron”, a name carried down through Indo-Caribbean families. It is not the same as true saffron from crocus flowers.
Where can I buy fresh turmeric in the UK?
Fresh turmeric root is sold in many Caribbean and Asian grocers, larger supermarkets and online.
